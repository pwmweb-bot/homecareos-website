What the Care Inspectorate assesses, and what you can evidence

Scotland grades on a six-point scale, and the lowest grade tends to set the tone. Here are the quality indicators that apply to a care at home service and what homecareOS can put behind each.

The framework

14 quality indicators apply to a domiciliary service

the Care Inspectorate’s quality framework for care at home, across four key questions assessed for care at home. homecareOS holds all of them, and applies the set that matches your agency’s nation.

5Wellbeing
4Leadership
3Staffing
2Planning

Grading runs 6 Excellent, 5 Very Good, 4 Good, 3 Adequate, 2 Weak, 1 Unsatisfactory. Separately, the Requirements for Care Services Regulations impose a written personal plan within 28 days, reviewed at least every six months, with a duty to notify the person of any revision.

What you can evidence

11 of the 14 have an automatic evidence route

Records you already keep are matched to the quality indicators they evidence, as they are filed. Nobody tags anything.

The platform derives evidence from fourteen concepts: risk management, medicines safety, staff training, supervision, safe recruitment, safeguarding, infection prevention, governance and audit, complaints handling, person-centred planning, family involvement, people’s experience, outcomes and goals, and falls and mobility.

Each concept is mapped to the quality indicators it evidences in your framework, so a completed training course, a falls risk assessment or a right-to-work check lands against Care Inspectorate’s wording rather than another regulator’s. For Scotland that mapping reaches 11 of 14 (79%).

The 3 with no automatic route, and they are worth reading as a set:

  • Vision and values positively inform practice
  • Leaders collaborate to support people
  • Staffing arrangements are right and staff work well together

Nearly every one is about culture, leadership or partnership working. That is not a gap we forgot to fill. It is the boundary of what any record system can evidence, and an inspector will ask you about them directly.

Where software stops

Three things homecareOS will not do for you

Worth saying plainly, because the alternative is you finding out at inspection.

Being well led

One requirement in our sample said simply that senior leadership must ensure managers and staff are well led. There is no field for that.

Team meetings and culture

Findings about team meetings not happening, or staff not working well together, are management practice.

A repeat finding

An area for improvement marked “not met” at the next inspection is a failure to act, not a failure to record.

And the one that should give any vendor pause. In the reports we read, several services that were marked down already had care software. One CIW finding records an electronic call monitoring system whose “full potential has not been realised”. One CQC finding is about an eMAR that allowed staff to make recording errors. Buying a system is not the same as being inspection ready, and we would rather say so than let you discover it.
