Home / Features / Document Management

Every document your agency holds, in one place and properly locked down

Staff files, client records and your own agency paperwork, filed where the work happens, with expiry dates tracked and access enforced by the database rather than by good intentions.

One System

Three filing cabinets, one place to look

Most agencies keep staff files in one drawer, client paperwork in another, and the agency's own certificates in a folder on somebody's desktop. All three live here, and each document is attached to the thing it belongs to.

The staff file

Everything you need to evidence that a carer was safe to send into someone's home.

  • Contracts, job descriptions and application forms
  • Background checks and right to work
  • Training certificates and qualifications
  • References, supervisions and appraisals
  • Driving licences, photo ID and proof of address
  • Fit notes, sickness notes and, where they exist, disciplinary and grievance records

The client record

The paperwork that sits behind a care package, kept with the service user it concerns.

  • Signed care plans and risk assessments
  • Consent forms
  • Hospital discharge letters and GP letters
  • Social worker reports
  • Mental capacity assessments and best interest decisions
  • DoLS authorisations
  • Incident reports and safeguarding referrals

The agency's own records

The documents an inspector asks for on the day, not the ones you have to go and find.

  • Your regulator registration certificate
  • Insurance certificates
  • Policies and procedures
  • Templates and meeting minutes
Before, not after

The documents that expire are the ones that catch people out

A background check, a right to work document, a training certificate, a driving licence, an insurance policy. Every one of them has a date on it, and every one of them is a problem the moment it passes without anyone noticing.

Any document here can carry an expiry date, and those dates are surfaced rather than buried. The question stops being "is anything out of date?" and becomes "what expires next month?", which is a question you can actually act on.

Because background check wording differs across the UK, what you see follows your nation: DBS in England and Wales, PVG in Scotland, AccessNI in Northern Ireland.

Access Control

Who can open a file is decided by the database

This is the part worth being specific about, because "secure" on its own means very little.

Scoped to your agency, in the storage layer

Every file in the document store is partitioned by agency, and reads, uploads, changes and deletions are each restricted the same way. Somebody signed in to a different agency cannot reach your files, and that is enforced underneath the application rather than by a check the application is trusted to remember.

Confidential documents stay confidential

Some things in a staff file are not for general viewing. Documents can be marked confidential, so sensitive material such as a disciplinary or grievance record is handled differently from a training certificate.

Attached to the work, not dumped in a folder

A document belongs to a carer, a service user, the agency, or a specific visit or incident. A wound photo sits with the incident it evidences. A signed care plan sits with the person it is about. Nothing lives in a shared drive that nobody has tidied since 2023.

Less Chasing

Applicants file their own paperwork

Recruitment is where document admin really bites. Candidates get their own login and upload right to work, ID and qualification documents themselves, into a separate applicant store, so nobody on your team is downloading attachments out of an inbox and renaming them by hand.

When someone is hired, you are not starting their staff file from nothing. See how the recruitment pipeline handles it end to end.

Four Nations

Your regulator, not just England's

The agency's nation drives what you see. A Welsh agency sees CIW, a Scottish agency sees the Care Inspectorate, a Northern Irish agency sees RQIA, and an English agency sees CQC. Registration certificates, background check wording and compliance labels all follow the same rule.

More on how that works across the compliance tooling.
