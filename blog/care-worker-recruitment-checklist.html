Care Worker Recruitment Checklist

A practical checklist for recruiting care workers in England: right to work, DBS, references and a health questionnaire, tied to Regulation 19 and Schedule 3.

By Philip Martin · Founder, HomecareOS · Published 6 November 2026

When a home care agency in England employs a care worker, safer recruitment is not optional. Regulation 19 of the Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014 is the fit and proper persons employed duty. Schedule 3 lists information required in respect of persons employed. CQC's guidance on Regulation 19 is the inspector's reading of that duty.

This checklist is for registered managers. It focuses on four items that often go missing or incomplete: right to work, DBS, references and a health questionnaire. It sits beside our longer guides on how to recruit care workers, applicant tracking and DBS checks. It is not legal advice, and it does not replace your recruitment policy.

1. Right to work

Before someone starts work, you must carry out a right to work check using an accepted method. The Home Office publishes an employer's guide to right to work checks and a practical page on checking a job applicant's right to work. The legal right to work in the UK page is the citizen-facing counterpart.

Checklist:

  • [ ] You know which check method you will use (for example a manual document check, or an online share-code check where eligible).
  • [ ] You have seen the original documents or completed the online check as the guidance requires, not a casual photo in a chat app.
  • [ ] You have kept a clear copy or the online check result, with the date of the check.
  • [ ] You know any expiry or follow-up date, and who will repeat a time-limited check.
  • [ ] The check is filed against the person, ready for inspection.

A right to work check is separate from a DBS check. Passing one does not satisfy the other.

2. DBS

Most care worker roles that involve personal care in someone's home are eligible for an enhanced DBS check, often with a check of the adults' barred list where the work is regulated activity. You decide eligibility from the role. Use the DBS eligibility collection and the Find out which DBS check tool when the job description is unclear. The process for applicants is summarised on DBS checks: applicant guidance.

Checklist:

  • [ ] The job description supports the level of check you are applying for.
  • [ ] Identity documents for the DBS application match the person.
  • [ ] You have a written position on starting work before the certificate arrives, with supervision if your policy allows any start at all.
  • [ ] When the certificate arrives, you record the name, certificate number, type/level, issue date, whether the barred list was checked, and your recruitment decision.
  • [ ] You know your retention rules. DBS guidance on handling certificate information, and CQC's inspection needs, both matter here; see our DBS guide.
  • [ ] If you use the Update Service, you have consent and a diary for status checks.

3. References

Schedule 3 expects, so far as reasonably practicable, satisfactory evidence of conduct in previous employment concerned with the provision of services relating to health or social care, and satisfactory verification of why that employment ended. CQC's recruitment policy and procedures supporting document expects your policy to cover references and how you check why previous employment ended.

Checklist:

  • [ ] You have asked for a full employment history, with written explanations for gaps.
  • [ ] You have requested references that cover recent and relevant care employment, not only a friend.
  • [ ] You have chased outstanding references before unsupervised work begins, in line with your policy.
  • [ ] You have recorded whether each reference was satisfactory, and any follow-up questions you asked.
  • [ ] Where a previous care employer will not reply, you have recorded what you did instead, so far as reasonably practicable.
  • [ ] HomecareOS users: the apply portal collects two references as part of the applicant pack; still judge whether they meet Schedule 3 for that person's history.

4. Health questionnaire

Regulation 19 is concerned with whether the person can perform the work for which they are employed. Agencies usually gather that information through a health questionnaire appropriate to the role, then follow up where answers raise a concern. It is not a fishing exercise for irrelevant medical detail, and it is not a substitute for occupational health advice when you need it.

Checklist:

  • [ ] The questionnaire is proportionate to the care worker role (moving and handling, lone working, infection risk, nights, and so on).
  • [ ] It is completed before appointment, or before unsupervised work, as your policy requires.
  • [ ] Someone competent reviews the answers; concerns are escalated, not filed unread.
  • [ ] Reasonable adjustments are considered and recorded where relevant.
  • [ ] The form sits on the staff file with the rest of the Schedule 3 information.

Put the four together before the first solo visit

A simple gate before unsupervised domiciliary work:

  1. Right to work complete and dated.
  2. Identity and photograph on file.
  3. Employment history with gaps explained.
  4. References satisfactory so far as reasonably practicable.
  5. DBS at the correct level seen and decision recorded (or a documented interim arrangement your policy allows).
  6. Health questionnaire reviewed.
  7. Induction and shadowing plan agreed, with mandatory training timed to the work.

Frequently asked questions

What does Regulation 19 require when we employ a care worker?

Regulation 19 requires that people employed for a regulated activity are of good character, have the necessary qualifications, competence, skills and experience, and have been subject to the necessary checks. Schedule 3 lists much of the information that must be available.

How many references do we need?

Schedule 3 expects satisfactory evidence of conduct in previous employment concerned with health or social care, so far as reasonably practicable, and verification of why that employment ended. HomecareOS collects two references through the apply portal. Your policy should say what "satisfactory" means.

Is a health questionnaire mandatory?

You need to be satisfied the person is physically and mentally fit for the work after reasonable adjustments. A role-appropriate health questionnaire is the usual way agencies gather that information before appointment. It is not a substitute for occupational health advice where the answers raise a concern.

Can HomecareOS invite applicants by SMS?

No. Invites are by email only. Applicants then complete the online apply portal.

Does conversion to a carer put them on the rota?

No. The carer record is ready to be added to the rota. Adding them is a separate step.
