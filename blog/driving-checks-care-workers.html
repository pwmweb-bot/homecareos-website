Driving Checks for Care Workers: Licence, Insurance, MOT

Most home care runs depend on carers driving their own cars between visits. That makes a carer's licence, insurance and car part of your recruitment checks, not an afterthought. This guide covers what to check, how to check it with the DVLA, and how to keep it up to date.

By Philip Martin · Founder, HomecareOS · Published 24 November 2026

In a care home, nobody asks whether a new carer can drive. In domiciliary care it is often one of the first questions. Rural runs, double-up calls in different villages and tight call windows usually mean carers drive their own cars from visit to visit, all day.

That makes driving a real part of the job. A carer with a disqualified licence, no business use insurance or a car with no MOT is a risk to themselves, to other road users and to the people waiting for their call. It is also a risk to your agency.

This guide is for registered managers and recruitment leads. It covers what the law and HSE expect, how to do a DVLA licence check with a check code, what to ask about insurance and the car, and how to keep it all current after the carer starts. It is general guidance, not legal or insurance advice.

Why driving is part of safer recruitment

CQC's guidance on Regulation 19: Fit and proper persons employed says providers must operate robust recruitment procedures, including any relevant checks, and must have a procedure for ongoing monitoring so staff remain able to do the job. If a role needs a driver, a valid licence is plainly one of those relevant checks.

Health and safety law adds a second layer. HSE's guidance for employers on driving and riding safely for work says:

  • health and safety law applies to work activities on the road in the same way as on a fixed site
  • the law applies to grey fleet vehicles, meaning vehicles owned and driven by workers for business purposes, and to cars used under cash allowance schemes
  • as part of your health and safety arrangements you must do a risk assessment, looking at the journey, the driver and the vehicle
  • commuting is not generally driving for work, but driving between clients is

For most home care agencies, almost every work vehicle is grey fleet. That is why the checks below matter: you do not own the cars, but you are still responsible for managing the risk.

Our care worker recruitment checklist covers the other pre-employment checks. Driving checks sit alongside them, not instead of them.

Decide which roles need a driver

Start by being clear about which roles actually need one. Some town-centre runs can be walked, and some carers work only on double-up calls with a colleague who drives. Writing "driver essential" on every advert narrows your pool for no reason, which matters when you are already finding it hard to recruit. Our guide to writing a care worker job advert covers how to say this clearly.

Where driving is essential, say so in the advert and the job description. Where it is not, consider whether you can build runs that work for non-drivers. A clear yes or no at the start saves time for everyone.

How to check a care worker's driving licence

The DVLA runs two GOV.UK services that work together. There is no need to photocopy a paper counterpart, which no longer exists.

Step 1: the carer creates a check code. On GOV.UK's View or share your driving licence information service, the carer enters their driving licence number, National Insurance number and the postcode on their licence. They can see their own record, including the vehicles they can drive and any penalty points or disqualifications, and create a check code to share it. GOV.UK says the code is valid for 21 days. The service covers licences issued in England, Wales and Scotland.

Step 2: you check the record. On Check someone's driving licence information, you enter the check code and the last 8 characters of their driving licence number. GOV.UK says you must use the code within 21 days and it can only be used once, so you need a new code for every check.

By post. If the carer cannot use the online service, GOV.UK says you and the driver can complete form D888/1 instead.

Two rules to build into your process:

  • Get permission first. GOV.UK says it is a criminal offence to obtain someone else's personal information without their permission. The carer creating and sharing the code is how they give it. Record the date of each check.
  • Look at the photocard too. Check the name, photo and expiry date on the card match the person and the DVLA record.

What to look for on the record

  • Entitlement. Can they drive a car with a full licence, not just a provisional one?
  • Penalty points and endorsements. GOV.UK's endorsement codes and penalty points page explains each code. Points stay on a record for 4 or 11 years depending on the offence.
  • Disqualifications. A current disqualification means they cannot drive for work. Full stop.
  • New drivers. GOV.UK's new drivers page says a licence is cancelled if the driver gets 6 or more points within 2 years of passing their test. A newly qualified carer with a few points is closer to losing their licence than an experienced driver with the same number.

Decide in your policy how you will treat points. A single minor speeding offence is not the same as a drink driving or careless driving conviction. Agree who makes the decision, and be consistent. HSE lists "driving record" and "any driving or riding offences" among the things to consider when choosing workers or allocating work, in its guidance on making sure drivers and riders are safe.

Business use insurance

GOV.UK's vehicle insurance page says you must have motor insurance to drive on UK roads, with third party cover as the legal minimum. That only sets the floor. The question for a care agency is whether the carer's policy covers driving for work.

HSE's guidance on making sure vehicles are safe says workers using privately owned vehicles for work must do checks on their vehicles, have them serviced, and have insurance and a valid MOT. In practice, ask each carer who drives to:

  • show you their certificate of motor insurance, with their name on it as a driver
  • confirm with their insurer that the policy covers business use for travelling between clients, as social and commuting cover may not
  • tell you the renewal date, and send the new certificate when it renews

GOV.UK's check if a vehicle is taxed page also points to askMID, the Motor Insurance Database service, for checking whether a vehicle is insured.

If a carer will ever drive a client, for example to an appointment or the shops, check that the insurance and your own policies cover it, and record it in the client's care plan and risk assessment.

MOT, tax and roadworthiness

Two GOV.UK services cover most of the vehicle checks with just the number plate:

  • Check the MOT history of a vehicle shows past results, mileage at each test and when the next MOT is due.
  • Check if a vehicle is taxed shows whether the car has up-to-date vehicle tax or is declared off the road (SORN).

HSE points out that an MOT only covers basic defects and does not guarantee a vehicle is safe. Its guidance asks employers to have a clear policy that unsafe vehicles should not be driven, and to make sure workers carry out routine checks such as lights, tyres and wheel fixings and report faults. For a carer that can be a simple weekly check, and a promise to ring the office rather than drive a car with a warning light on.

Fitness to drive and eyesight

GOV.UK's guide to health conditions and driving says drivers must tell the DVLA about some medical conditions, and can be fined up to £1,000 if they do not. Its driving eyesight rules say drivers must wear glasses or contact lenses every time they drive if they need them to meet the standard.

You do not need to ask carers for their medical history to manage this. Put it in your driving policy instead: carers who drive for work must meet the DVLA's rules, and must tell you if the DVLA restricts or removes their licence. HSE's guidance for workers who drive for work says workers should tell their employer if they are taking medication that may affect their ability to drive.

Journeys, rotas and phones

The HSE risk assessment covers the journey as well as the driver and car. For home care, the journey is your rota. HSE's guidance on planning and managing journeys says employers should calculate journey times that allow safe driving within the speed limit, and make sure company policy does not put drivers under pressure to take risks because of agreed arrival times. It also says sleep-related incidents are most likely between 2am and 6am and between 2pm and 4pm.

In practice that means:

  • realistic travel time between visits, not back-to-back calls ten miles apart
  • care with early starts after late bed calls, which our guide to care worker shift patterns covers
  • a clear rule on phones. GOV.UK's page on using a phone when driving says it is illegal to hold and use a phone, sat nav or similar device while driving, including when stopped at traffic lights or queuing. Carers should pull over safely before checking a carer app or calling the office.

Travel time also affects pay. Our guide to National Minimum Wage and travel time in home care explains why time spent travelling between visits counts as working time.

Mileage payments

Mileage is part of your offer to carers, and it has tax rules. GOV.UK's guidance on business travel mileage for employees' own vehicles lists the approved mileage allowance rates you can pay without reporting to HMRC. For cars and vans it shows 55p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles from 6 April 2026 (45p before that date) and 25p per mile above 10,000. If you pay more, the excess has to be reported and taxed. Check the current page before you set or advertise a rate.

A simple driving checks process

  1. At application. Ask whether the applicant drives and has access to a car for work, and whether the role needs it.
  2. Before the offer is confirmed. Run a DVLA licence check using the carer's check code, look at the photocard, and record the date and outcome.
  3. Before the first solo run. See the insurance certificate with business use confirmed, check the MOT and tax online, and record expiry dates.
  4. At induction. Go through your driving policy: phones, breaks, vehicle checks, reporting incidents and near misses, and telling you about points or medical changes. HSE suggests covering work-related road safety at induction.
  5. Ongoing. Recheck licences at the interval in your policy, chase insurance and MOT renewals before they expire, and review the risk assessment when something changes.

Keep driving records with the rest of the carer's file, so an inspector can see the checks were done and kept up to date. Our guide to onboarding new carers covers where these steps fit between offer and first visit.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check a care worker's driving licence?

Ask the carer to create a licence check code on GOV.UK's View or share your driving licence information service, then enter it with the last 8 characters of their licence number on GOV.UK's Check someone's driving licence information service. The code lasts 21 days and works once. You can also do the check by post with form D888/1.

Can I check a carer's licence without their permission?

No. GOV.UK says it is a criminal offence to obtain someone else's personal information without their permission. The check code system works because the carer creates the code and gives it to you.

Does a carer need business use car insurance?

HSE says workers who use their own vehicles for work must have insurance and a valid MOT. Ordinary social and commuting cover may not include driving between clients, so ask carers to confirm with their insurer that the policy covers business use, and keep evidence on file.

Is the agency responsible if a carer drives their own car for work?

HSE says health and safety law applies to work activities on the road as it does on a fixed site, including grey fleet vehicles owned and driven by workers for business. You must include driving in your risk assessment, covering the journey, the driver and the vehicle.

How often should we recheck licences and insurance?

There is no single legal interval. HSE asks employers to make sure licences, insurance and MOTs are legal and up to date, so set an interval in your policy, recheck when insurance or MOT dates expire, and ask carers to tell you straight away about new penalty points, a disqualification or a medical condition that affects driving.

What mileage rate can we pay carers without reporting it to HMRC?

GOV.UK lists the approved mileage allowance rate for cars and vans as 55p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles from 6 April 2026 (45p before), and 25p above that. Paying more than the approved amount has tax and reporting consequences, so check the GOV.UK page before setting your rate.
