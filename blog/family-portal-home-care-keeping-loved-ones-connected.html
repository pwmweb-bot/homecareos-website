New Family Portal: Keeping Loved Ones Connected

Families can now view visit logs, care plans, and real-time updates through our secure family portal. Transparency builds trust between agencies and the families they serve, and it materially reduces the volume of "did the carer turn up?" calls hitting the office.

By Philip Martin · Founder, homecareOS · Published 3 March 2026

The 11pm phone call problem

Every home care coordinator we've worked with has told us about the 11pm phone call. It usually goes: "Hi, this is Sarah, my dad has Parkinson's, you visited him this morning, sorry to call so late, but he's a bit confused tonight, can you tell me what time the carer was there and whether he took his medication?"

That call shouldn't exist. The information Sarah needs (visit time, carer, medication confirmation) already lives in the platform. It's been there since the carer tapped "complete" on their phone at 09:47am. The fact that a family member is calling at 11pm to find it out manually is a software problem, not a customer service problem.

The new Family Portal closes that gap. Sarah opens an app on her phone, sees that her dad was visited by Beth between 09:30 and 09:47, that meds were given on time, and that mood and appetite were both flagged green. The 11pm call doesn't happen. The coordinator's evening doesn't get interrupted. Sarah trusts the agency more, not less, because the answer was instantly available.

What the Family Portal shows

Three default information levels, set by the service user (or their authorised representative):

LevelVisibleTypical use
Visit-onlyToday's planned visits, arrival window, assigned carer name. Real-time check-in/check-out confirmations.Distant relatives who want reassurance the visit happened.
StandardVisit-only, plus: daily care summaries, medication confirmation, missed-visit alerts, mood/wellbeing observations, contact channel.The default for most adult children of service users.
FullStandard, plus: body-map visibility, full care plan, observations, incident notifications.The designated next-of-kin or LPA holder.

The level is set per family member, not per service user, so Sarah can have Standard while her cousin in Australia has Visit-only. Consent is recorded against the service user's record and is fully revocable.

The missed-visit alert is the killer feature

Of everything the portal does, the feature families consistently say matters most is the missed-visit alert. The agency may already know within five minutes that a visit hasn't happened. The family knowing within five minutes (through a clear push notification, not a panicked phone call) is the difference between trust and not trust.

The alert says exactly three things: "Visit missed at [time], [service user], [reason if known]. Office is contacting you in [n] minutes with cover details." No corporate hedging language, no "we are aware of an issue and investigating". The family already knows. They just want to know the agency does too, and what's happening next.

The carer profile builds trust

Family members tell us they want to know who is in the house. Not in a controlling way, just normal "is this person trustworthy and competent" instinct. The Family Portal shows a brief profile for each carer assigned to a visit: first name, photo (with consent), languages spoken, brief bio, training relevant to this service user (e.g. "trained in PEG feeding"), and how long they've been with the agency.

This isn't surveillance. It's normal context. Families don't ask for it most of the time. But on the day a new carer is assigned for the first time, knowing the carer's name and seeing a friendly face on screen before they arrive at mum's door reduces "stranger anxiety" enormously.

A note on what we deliberately don't show

The Family Portal isn't a surveillance tool. We made deliberate design decisions to keep it on the trust side of the line:

  • No live tracking of carer location. Families see arrival and departure times, not where the carer is between visits. That's the carer's working day, not the family's business.
  • No carer rating system. Families can flag a concern through a structured channel that goes to the registered manager, but no public rating. We've seen "rate your carer" features in other software and they're wrong, they create perverse incentives and undermine the carer-service-user relationship.
  • No video by default. Some Premium tier families opt into pre-recorded video updates from the carer (e.g. "your mum did her exercises this morning, here she is"). It's strictly opt-in on both sides: the carer chooses whether to send, the family chooses whether to receive.

What this changes operationally

For agencies that have rolled out the portal, three measurable things tend to shift:

  1. "Did the carer turn up?" calls drop materially. The information is in the portal; the calls don't need to happen. Office volume goes down without anyone losing visibility.
  2. Trust-based complaints drop. Most home care complaints aren't about care quality. They're about communication. When the family knows what's happening in real time, the residual complaint rate falls noticeably.
  3. Carer recognition goes up. Families seeing carer names and photos consistently leads to more spontaneous thank-yous, christmas cards, and reviews. These are small things, but they materially affect carer wellbeing and retention.

None of this requires the family to have premium. The standard Family Portal is included in every agency plan, including the free starter tier (up to 5 active carers and 25 service users). The Premium tier exists for distant relatives who want richer features and is paid by the family, not the agency.

How agencies have rolled it out

The patterns that work best in our experience:

  1. Pilot with 5-10 service users whose families are tech-positive. Get the consent process right, fix any teething issues.
  2. Roll out by service user as part of new-joiner onboarding. Don't try to backfill all 60 service users at once. Let consent come naturally as part of the introductions.
  3. Send a one-page family welcome explaining what the portal shows, what it doesn't, and how consent works. Avoid corporate language; write it in plain English.
  4. Set expectations clearly: the portal is to keep families informed, not to replace conversations with the registered manager when something needs discussing.
  5. Track adoption. Families using the portal consistently tend to be the families with the highest care-experience scores.

Frequently asked questions

What does the Family Portal show?

Family members with consent see: today's planned visits, real-time arrival/departure confirmations, daily care summaries, medication confirmation, missed-visit alerts within minutes, the carer profile for each visit, and a contact channel back to the office.

Is the Family Portal free for families?

The standard Family Portal is included in every agency plan (including the free starter tier). A Family Premium subscription (£4.99/month, paid by the family) adds richer features like photo updates, body-map visibility, and longer message history.

How quickly are missed-visit alerts sent?

The moment the visit window closes without a check-in, typically within 5 minutes. The alert goes to family contacts and the office simultaneously, naming the visit, the service user, and what happens next.

What's the difference between the Family Portal and a "client portal"?

A client portal is typically read-only and designed for the service user or one next-of-kin. The Family Portal supports whole-family engagement: multiple consented family members at different access levels, real-time push notifications, mobile-first design.

How is family consent managed?

Each service user (or authorised representative) decides which family members get portal access and at what level (Visit-only / Standard / Full). Consent is recorded with date and signature, and can be withdrawn or changed at any time.
