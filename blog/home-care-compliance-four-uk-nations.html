Home Care Compliance in the Four UK Nations: CQC, CIW, Care Inspectorate and RQIA Compared

One country, four regulators. England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland each regulate domiciliary care with their own inspectorate, their own framework, their own workforce rules and their own background checks. This guide puts the four regimes side by side, then points you to the deep-dive guide for your nation.

By Philip Martin · Founder, homecareOS · Published 25 August 2026
Home care compliance in the UK is split across four separate regulators, one per nation. The Care Quality Commission (CQC) inspects English domiciliary agencies against the Single Assessment Framework and its 34 Quality Statements. Care Inspectorate Wales (CIW) inspects Welsh services under RISCA and its four quality themes. The Care Inspectorate grades Scottish care at home services on a six-point scale against the Health and Social Care Standards. RQIA registers and inspects Northern Ireland agencies under the 2007 Regulations and the DHSSPS Minimum Standards. Workforce rules diverge too: Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland each run a mandatory care-worker register (SCW, SSSC and NISCC respectively) while England has none, and background checks split between DBS, PVG and AccessNI. homecareOS covers all four frameworks in one platform: set your agency's nation once and the right regulator templates, register fields and vetting checks apply automatically.

Search for "home care compliance UK" and most of what comes back is really home care compliance England. The CQC, the Single Assessment Framework, the Care Act, DBS checks: all of it stops at the borders with Wales and Scotland and at the Irish Sea. If your agency is in Cardiff, Glasgow or Belfast, or if you operate services in more than one nation, the English material describes someone else's inspection.

We've written a full guide to each nation's regime. This page is the map that connects them: what each regulator actually inspects, how the workforce rules differ, a comparison table you can keep, and what all of it means if you run services across a border. England: CQC and the Single Assessment Framework

English domiciliary agencies register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC) and are inspected under the Single Assessment Framework, in place since 2023. The framework keeps the five familiar Key Questions (Safe, Effective, Caring, Responsive, Well-led) and breaks them into 34 Quality Statements, each with evidence categories attached. Ratings run Outstanding, Good, Requires Improvement, Inadequate.

Ahead of inspection, CQC asks for a Provider Information Return (PIR): a structured submission of narrative and data against the Quality Statements. Background checks run through the DBS. What England notably lacks is a mandatory register for the general care workforce; there is no English equivalent of SCW, SSSC or NISCC, so workforce evidence rests on training records, supervision and DBS currency instead of a registration number. The full detail, including what inspectors actually ask on the day, is in our CQC compliance guide. New providers should also see the CQC registration checklist.

Wales: CIW, RISCA and Social Care Wales

In Wales the regulator is Care Inspectorate Wales (CIW), inspecting under the Regulation and Inspection of Social Care (Wales) Act 2016, known as RISCA. Services are assessed against four quality themes: Well-being, Care and Support, Environment, and Leadership and Management, with the person-centred duties of the Social Services and Well-being (Wales) Act 2014 running through all of them.

Two things set Wales apart. First, the workforce: domiciliary care workers must register with Social Care Wales (SCW) and keep that registration current, while criminal-record checks still run through the DBS, the same scheme as England. Second, governance: every service has a designated Responsible Individual with quarterly visit duties and a six-monthly quality review, and every agency carries the Welsh language Active Offer, a care-records duty with no English counterpart. The Wales guide covers each in practical detail.

Scotland: the Care Inspectorate, SSSC and PVG

Scottish care at home services register with the Care Inspectorate and are inspected against the Health and Social Care Standards, using a quality framework built around key questions and a six-point grading scale: from 6 (Excellent) down to 1 (Unsatisfactory). It is a genuinely different scoring system from CQC's four-word ratings, and self-evaluation is expected in that language.

The workforce is individually regulated: care at home workers must register with the Scottish Social Services Council (SSSC), hold or work towards the relevant qualification, and record Post Registration Training and Learning across each registration period. Background checking runs through the Protecting Vulnerable Groups (PVG) scheme operated by Disclosure Scotland, not the DBS. Add the mandatory annual return and event notifications and Scotland's regime is arguably the most administratively distinct of the four. The Scotland guide walks through all of it.

Northern Ireland: RQIA, NISCC and AccessNI

In Northern Ireland the Regulation and Quality Improvement Authority (RQIA) registers and inspects domiciliary care agencies under the Domiciliary Care Agencies Regulations (Northern Ireland) 2007, with the DHSSPS Minimum Standards setting out what inspectors expect to see. A named registered manager is accountable for the day-to-day running of the agency, and providers must run ongoing quality monitoring, produce reports on it, and notify RQIA of deaths, serious incidents and allegations of abuse.

The workforce registers with the Northern Ireland Social Care Council (NISCC), each carer holding a PIN with a renewal date, and vetting runs through AccessNI, typically at Enhanced level for care workers in regulated activity. A DBS certificate is not the correct record for an NI care role. The Northern Ireland guide has the full picture.

Operating across borders: what changes and what doesn't

A growing number of providers run services in more than one nation: an English agency opening a branch in Wrexham, a Scottish provider expanding into Carlisle, a group with services either side of the Irish Sea. The mistake to avoid is assuming your existing registration travels with you. It doesn't. Each nation's service is registered with, and answers to, that nation's regulator, and the frameworks do not cross-recognise each other's ratings or evidence formats.

In practice, three things change at the border and one thing stays the same.

  • The framework changes. The same care plan has to answer Quality Statements in England, the four themes in Wales, the key questions and grades in Scotland, and the Minimum Standards in Northern Ireland.
  • The workforce record changes. A carer moving from your Chester branch to your Wrexham branch needs SCW registration before delivering care in Wales. Scottish staff need PVG membership, not a DBS certificate; Northern Irish staff need AccessNI and a NISCC PIN.
  • The reporting calendar changes. PIR deadlines, RI quarterly visits, Scottish annual returns and RQIA quality reports each run on their own cycle.

What stays the same is the daily discipline underneath. Every regime ultimately inspects the same raw material: visits delivered, care notes written, medications recorded, incidents handled, training kept current. Capture that evidence once, as a by-product of normal work, and the remaining job is mapping it to the right framework, which is exactly the job software should be doing for you.

Frequently asked questions

Who regulates home care in each UK nation?

England: the CQC, against the Single Assessment Framework. Wales: CIW, under RISCA. Scotland: the Care Inspectorate, against the Health and Social Care Standards. Northern Ireland: RQIA, under the 2007 Regulations and the DHSSPS Minimum Standards. None of the four has jurisdiction outside its own nation.

Do care workers have to register with a professional body?

In Wales (SCW), Scotland (SSSC) and Northern Ireland (NISCC), yes: registration is mandatory and must be kept current. England has no equivalent register for the general care workforce, so English agencies evidence competence through training, supervision and DBS currency instead.

Is a DBS check valid across the whole UK?

No. The DBS covers England and Wales. Scotland uses the PVG scheme through Disclosure Scotland, and Northern Ireland uses AccessNI, typically at Enhanced level for care roles. Cross-border agencies need the right check, and the right record fields, for each nation.

Can one software platform handle all four regulators?

Only if nation is a real configuration choice. homecareOS applies CQC, CIW, Care Inspectorate or RQIA frameworks based on your agency's nation, with the matching workforce register and vetting fields. England-first platforms that only render CQC dashboards leave agencies elsewhere translating evidence by hand.

What if my agency operates in more than one nation?

Each nation's service is its own regulatory entity: separate registration, separate framework, different workforce rules. Keep the daily evidence discipline identical everywhere and let each service map that evidence to its own regulator's framework and reporting cycle.
