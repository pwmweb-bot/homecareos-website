How UK Home Care Agencies Are Cutting Admin Time by 60%

Read about the journey from paper-based processes to a fully digital operation: where the time actually goes, what good software automates, what doesn't change, and the realistic playbook for getting there.

By Philip Martin · Founder, homecareOS · Published 17 February 2026

The 60% number: where it actually comes from

Sixty per cent admin time reduction is the kind of headline that sounds like marketing. But it's a credible number for agencies moving from paper-based or partially-digital operations to a properly integrated platform, because most home care admin time isn't strictly necessary; it's just the cost of moving information between systems.

Before walking through where the savings come from, an honest framing: this is about non-care admin time. Direct care doesn't change. The carer still spends 30 minutes with the service user, still helps with personal care, still administers medication, still writes notes about the visit. The 60% reduction is the office-side and coordinator-side admin work that connects all that up, not the work of caring itself.

Where the admin time actually goes (typical paper agency)

If you sit in a typical paper-based UK home care agency office for a week and time-track the work, you see roughly this distribution:

TaskTime per week (typical 25-carer agency)
Building / adjusting the rota15-20 hours
Phoning around for cover when carers call in sick8-12 hours
Manually transcribing visit times into payroll4-6 hours
Manually transcribing visit data into invoices4-6 hours
Filing paper care notes / MAR3-5 hours
Compiling training compliance reports2-3 hours
Updating care plans (paper, often delayed)2-4 hours
"Did the carer turn up?" calls from families2-4 hours
Audit / PIR preparation when due20-40 hours per audit cycle

That's roughly 40-60 hours of office and coordinator time per week, before audit cycles. About two-thirds of it is information re-typing or information chasing, work that exists only because data is in too many places.

What disappears with proper digital tooling

The categories of admin work that essentially stop being necessary when an agency goes digital:

  1. Re-typing visit times into payroll. The carer's check-in/check-out times go straight into the payroll calculation. No re-keying.
  2. Re-typing visit data into invoices. Visits become invoice line items automatically based on payer-specific rate cards. The invoice generates itself; you review and send.
  3. Phoning around for cover. The AI cover finder ranks available carers in seconds; the coordinator calls the top match instead of trying to mentally track who's free.
  4. Hand-transcribing care notes. Notes typed on the carer's phone at the point of care are immediately available in the office.
  5. Compiling training compliance reports. Live dashboard, always current.
  6. Family "did the carer turn up?" calls. The Family Portal shows it. The calls don't happen.
  7. Manual PIR assembly. Most PIR data comes out of the platform with one click.

Not all of these go to zero. But each typically drops by 70-90% in real practice.

What doesn't change (be honest)

It's worth being honest about what stays roughly the same, or in some cases gets slightly more demanding:

  • Initial service user set-up. A digital care plan is more thorough than a paper one. Setting up a new service user takes the same time or slightly longer.
  • Care plan reviews. Easier to keep digital plans current, which means you actually do, and "actually doing it" is more work than "intending to do it next month".
  • Recruitment and HR. Software doesn't recruit carers for you. Digital training tracking helps you spot expiry, but the training itself still happens.
  • Direct service user contact. Phone calls, home visits, family conversations. These don't go away, and shouldn't.

The net effect is still strongly positive, but it's not "all admin disappears". It's "the wrong kind of admin disappears, and the right kind becomes easier to do well".

The realistic playbook

Across UK home care agencies that have successfully made the transition:

  1. Week 1-2: Choose your platform. Run two demos minimum. Talk to two existing customers minimum. Side-by-side comparisons help you narrow the field; demos confirm fit.
  2. Week 3: Set up agency, payers, services, rate cards. Migrate your service user list and carer list. Most platforms have data import tools.
  3. Week 4: Pilot with 1 coordinator and 5 carers. Fix all the little issues that appear. This week is uncomfortable; commit to it.
  4. Week 5-6: Roll out to full carer team. The carer mobile app is the part that needs most support. Book in 30-minute training sessions per carer.
  5. Week 7-8: Switch payroll and invoicing to the platform. Run one parallel period (paper + digital) to confirm matching numbers.
  6. Week 9-12: Care plans, audit cycles, family portal rollout, integrations. By this point the platform is the main system; paper is the backup.

Agencies that get past week 5 successfully rarely revert. Agencies that bail typically do so in week 2-3 because the demo didn't match reality, or in week 4-5 because the carer-app rollout was rushed. Both are avoidable with proper expectation-setting.

Where the savings actually go

Here's the part that matters: what does an agency do with the freed-up admin time? The honest pattern across agencies we've worked with:

  • Coordinators spend more time on actual coordination: relationship-building with carers, talking to service users, managing complex situations.
  • Registered managers spend more time on care quality: supervisions, observations, audit cycles done well.
  • Owners spend more time on growth: new contracts, partnerships, financial planning.
  • The agency takes on more service users with the same office headcount.

Notice what isn't in the list: "we made staff redundant". The agencies that report most strongly positive transitions don't cut headcount. They redirect time toward the work that needed doing all along but didn't get done because admin was eating the calendar.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 60% admin time reduction realistic?

For agencies moving from paper-based or partially-digital systems to a properly integrated platform, a 50-70% reduction in non-care administrative time is realistic and consistent with patterns observed across the sector. Direct care doesn't change.

What admin disappears completely when going digital?

Re-typing visit times into payroll, re-typing visit data into invoices, manually phoning around for cover, hand-transcribing care notes, manually compiling training reports, and manually preparing PIR submissions. These don't get faster. They stop being necessary work.

Where does new admin work appear?

Some modest increases: initial service user set-up (more thorough than paper), maintaining digital care plans (because they're easier to keep current), managing carer mobile-app access. The net is strongly positive but "digital" doesn't mean "no admin".

How long does the transition actually take?

For a small-to-medium UK domiciliary agency (5-30 carers), 4-8 weeks is typical. Larger agencies (50+) usually need 8-16 weeks. The first 30 days are the hardest. Agencies that get past day 30 rarely revert.

What's the first thing to digitise?

Visits and the rota, always. Scheduling is the highest-volume admin task. Once visits and rotas are digital, everything else follows naturally. Trying to digitise care plans before scheduling is a common mistake.
