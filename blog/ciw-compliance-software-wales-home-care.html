CIW Compliance Software: A Practical Guide for Welsh Home Care Agencies

If you run a domiciliary support service in Wales, you are inspected by Care Inspectorate Wales against RISCA, not the CQC. This guide walks through the four quality themes, the Statement of Purpose, Social Care Wales registration, the Responsible Individual's duties and the Welsh language Active Offer, and shows exactly how the right home care software carries each one.

By Philip Martin · Founder, homecareOS · Published 20 June 2026

Most software marketing aimed at UK home care is written for England. It talks about the CQC, the Single Assessment Framework and the Care Act as though they apply everywhere. In Wales, they do not. Choosing a platform on the strength of an English feature list is how Welsh agencies end up faking the evidence shape at inspection, and it is entirely avoidable. This is the companion to our England CQC compliance guide, written specifically for domiciliary services regulated in Wales.

First, the boundary: CQC and the Care Act do not apply in Wales

This matters enough to state plainly. The Care Quality Commission (CQC) and the Care Act 2014 are England-only. In Wales, home care is regulated by Care Inspectorate Wales (CIW) under the Regulation and Inspection of Social Care (Wales) Act 2016, RISCA for short, sitting alongside the person-centred duties of the Social Services and Well-being (Wales) Act 2014.

The practical consequences run right through your operation. Your inspection is structured around four quality themes, not five key questions. Your workforce must be registered with a body that has no English equivalent. And you carry a Welsh language duty that simply does not exist across the border. A platform that only knows how to render a CQC dashboard cannot produce any of this cleanly. So the first question to ask any vendor is not "do you do compliance?" It is "which regulator is this configured for?" If the honest answer is CQC only, the platform is not built for you. Our wider home care software buying checklist makes four-nation fit its second test for exactly this reason.

RISCA and the four quality themes

CIW inspects domiciliary support services against four quality themes. Everything you evidence should be organised so it falls naturally under one of them:

Quality themeWhat CIW is assessingWhere the software evidence lives
Well-beingWhether people achieve the personal outcomes that matter to them, with voice, choice and controlOutcome-focused care plans, goals, service user and family feedback
Care & SupportWhether care is planned, delivered and reviewed safely and in line with each personal planPersonal plans, daily notes, eMAR, body maps, visit completion, review dates
EnvironmentWhether care is delivered safely in people's own homes, including risk in the domiciliary settingHome risk assessments, equipment records, lone-working and access notes
Leadership & ManagementGovernance, workforce, quality assurance and the Responsible Individual's oversightTraining and SCW registration, supervisions, audits, incidents, RI reports

Well-being sits at the centre. Under the 2014 Act, a personal plan is not a task list. It records what a good day looks like for the person and how support helps them achieve it. Software that only captures visit times and completed tasks will underperform against the Well-being theme. You want digital care records that hold outcomes and preferences as first-class content, with review dates the system chases automatically.

The Statement of Purpose is your anchor document

Every registered service in Wales must produce and maintain a Statement of Purpose: what the service does, the range of care and support it provides, the outcomes it aims to achieve, staffing, and how it is run. CIW treats it as the benchmark. Inspectors check whether what actually happens on the ground matches what the Statement promises.

That places a quiet demand on your systems. If your Statement says you review each personal plan every three months, your software has to make those reviews happen and prove they happened. If it says Welsh-speaking support is available, your records have to show it being offered and delivered. The Statement of Purpose is not a document you write once and file. It is a set of commitments your day-to-day platform either keeps or exposes.

Social Care Wales registration, and why expiry tracking matters

This is one of the sharpest differences from England. Domiciliary care workers in Wales must register with Social Care Wales (SCW), the national workforce regulator, and keep that registration current through continuing professional development. England has no equivalent mandatory register for the general care workforce, so English-built software often has nowhere to store a registration number at all.

Alongside SCW registration, Welsh agencies still run DBS checks, the same disclosure service as England, unlike Scotland's PVG scheme or Northern Ireland's AccessNI. So a compliant Welsh workforce record needs to hold, per carer:

  • SCW registration number and renewal date, with an alert well before it lapses
  • DBS certificate and issue date, tracked to your re-check policy
  • Induction and mandatory training, including the All Wales Induction Framework, with expiry dates
  • Supervision and appraisal history, evidencing ongoing competence

An unregistered worker delivering care is a serious finding. The value of nation-aware software is that it holds the right identifiers for Wales and treats an approaching SCW renewal exactly like an approaching DBS expiry, as something to flag, not something to discover at inspection.

The Responsible Individual: quarterly visits and six-monthly reviews

RISCA requires every registered service to have a designated Responsible Individual (RI), an accountable person with legal duties for oversight of quality and safety. Two of those duties have direct software implications.

First, the RI must visit the service regularly, at least once each quarter, speaking with people who receive support and with staff, and recording what they find. Second, the provider must review the quality of care at least every six months, drawing together feedback, outcomes, complaints and audit findings into a report that feeds continuous improvement.

Both stand or fall on how quickly you can surface current evidence. If visit completion, missed-call patterns, medication errors, training compliance and complaint resolution are scattered across a rota tool, a spreadsheet and an inbox, every quarterly visit and every six-monthly review becomes a reconstruction exercise. If they live in one platform, the RI walks in with the numbers already assembled and spends the time on judgement instead of data-gathering. This is the same discipline that makes England's PIR manageable, reframed for the Welsh reporting cycle.

Welsh language: the Active Offer is a care-records duty

Welsh agencies carry a language duty that has no counterpart in England. Under the Welsh Language Standards and the Welsh Government's "More than just words" framework, social care providers are expected to make an Active Offer, providing a service in Welsh without the person having to ask for it. For many older people, and especially those living with dementia, Welsh is the language of comfort and safety; being met in English first is not a neutral choice.

This is where it becomes a records issue rather than a website issue. Meeting the Active Offer in practice means your software can:

  • Record each person's language preference as a structured field, not a note buried in free text
  • Help you match Welsh-speaking carers to people who want them when building the rota
  • Produce bilingual care documents and family updates where they are needed

CIW looks for evidence that the Active Offer is real. Software that treats language as a first-class attribute of the person, and carries it into scheduling and communications, is doing genuine compliance work, not ticking a box.

How nation-aware software actually carries CIW compliance

The principle behind homecareOS is that the nation is set once and drives everything after it. When a Welsh agency onboards, that choice configures the platform for CIW rather than CQC: the workforce record exposes an SCW registration field beside the DBS field; the compliance templates map to the four RISCA quality themes and Well-being outcomes; and the reporting is shaped for the RI's quarterly visits and six-monthly reviews rather than an English PIR. The same single-platform discipline that makes an inspection survivable (every visit, note, medication and audit captured as a by-product of normal work) applies identically in Wales. What changes is the regulatory frame it is poured into.

For the broader picture of how the four UK nations differ and what to look for across all of them, our complete UK home care software guide is the wider companion to this Wales-specific piece. And because CIW, CQC, the Care Inspectorate and RQIA each demand a different evidence shape, "built for home care, not care homes" and "built for your regulator, not just England" are the two questions worth pinning any vendor down on before you sign.

Frequently asked questions

Does the CQC regulate home care in Wales?

No. The CQC and the Care Act 2014 apply in England only. Domiciliary care in Wales is regulated by Care Inspectorate Wales (CIW) under RISCA, the Regulation and Inspection of Social Care (Wales) Act 2016. Software configured only for CQC will not produce the evidence a CIW inspector expects.

What is RISCA and what are the four quality themes?

RISCA is the Regulation and Inspection of Social Care (Wales) Act 2016. CIW inspects domiciliary services across four quality themes: Well-being, Care & Support, Environment, and Leadership & Management. Well-being reflects the person-centred duties of the Social Services and Well-being (Wales) Act 2014.

Do care workers in Wales need to register with Social Care Wales?

Yes. Domiciliary care workers in Wales must register with Social Care Wales (SCW) and keep that registration current. England has no equivalent register, so your software should hold each worker's SCW number and renewal date alongside their DBS check and alert you before either lapses.

What are the Responsible Individual's reporting duties?

Every registered service must have a Responsible Individual (RI). The RI must visit the service at least once each quarter, speaking with people and staff, and the provider must review the quality of care at least every six months. Both rely on being able to pull current visit, care and workforce data quickly.

What is the Welsh language Active Offer?

The Active Offer means providing a service in Welsh without someone having to ask, under the "More than just words" framework and the Welsh Language Standards. In practice it means recording language preference, matching Welsh-speaking carers, and producing bilingual records where needed, a care-quality duty CIW assesses, not just a website matter.
