How to Choose Home Care Software: A 12-Point Checklist for UK Agencies

Twelve concrete things to test before you commit to a UK home care platform, written so you can print it, take it to demos, and use it to compare vendors without falling for marketing. Each point includes what to look for, what good looks like, and the exact question to ask. If you only do one thing on this list, do point 8.

By Philip Martin · Founder, homecareOS · Published 30 May 2026

For the longer landscape view of what you're shopping for, see our complete guide to home care software in the UK. This post is the practical companion, the checklist you actually take into demos.

1. The scheduling engine

Why it matters: the rota is where every other feature gets used or ignored. A clunky scheduling engine wastes more coordinator hours per week than every other software problem combined.

What good looks like: drag-and-drop visit blocks on a calendar grid (not a list view that calls itself a rota), smooth performance with 60+ carers and 400+ weekly visits, run templates you can edit individually after applying, native handling of double-ups and shadow shifts as attendee roles rather than bolt-on flags, and a separate AI cover finder for last-minute absences.

Ask: "Can I see a demo with 80 carers and 500 visits in one week, on a phone-sized screen, dragged in real time?"

2. Four-nation regulatory fit

Why it matters: if you operate in Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland, a platform configured only for CQC in England will force you to fake the evidence shape. Inspections won't go well.

What good looks like: separate background-check field types for DBS / PVG / AccessNI; separate registration-body IDs for SCW / SSSC / NISCC; evidence templates aligned to the relevant regulator (CQC Key Questions, CIW RISCA themes, Care Inspectorate quality indicators, RQIA standards); Welsh-language considerations where applicable.

Ask: "Show me a screenshot of a worked compliance dashboard for my regulator." If they only have CQC examples, they're not configured for you.

3. Truly offline mobile app for field carers

Why it matters: UK home care visits regularly happen in basement flats, rural blackspots, and lift shafts. A mobile app that needs signal to record a note is a mobile app that loses care notes.

What good looks like: care notes, MAR signatures, body-map entries and check-in/out captured offline and synced reliably when signal returns. No data loss on app crash mid-visit. Service-user profile + care plan pre-cached for today's visits.

Ask: "Put the phone in airplane mode, record a full visit including a MAR sign, then come back online and show me the sync." If the answer is "it'll sync when reconnected" without a live demo, that's a yellow flag.

4. Pricing transparency and pricing model

Why it matters: UK home care has the highest office-to-field staff ratio of any care setting. Per-user pricing quietly punishes you for every required office hire. See our deep dive on per-user vs per-carer pricing for the worked example.

What good looks like: published pricing on the vendor's website (not "request a quote"). Clear answer to "what counts as a user". No surprise charges for family or commissioner logins. No per-service-user surprises when a council block contract doubles your caseload overnight.

Ask in writing: "What's my total cost over 24 months including implementation, modules, payroll integration, and any per-seat creep?" Get it in an email you can refer back to.

5. eMAR depth: controlled drugs, PRN, refusals

Why it matters: medication management is the single highest clinical-risk area in domiciliary care. A surface-level eMAR will pass a small-agency inspection and fail a serious incident.

What good looks like: controlled-drug double-sign workflow, PRN (as-required) recording with effectiveness follow-up, refusal recording with reason codes, photograph capture of medication labels at first administration, MAR reconciliation at the end of each cycle, and a medication-error log that doesn't pretend errors don't happen.

Ask: "Walk me through a controlled-drug administration end-to-end, including what the carer sees and what gets logged for audit."

6. A family portal that actually gets used

Why it matters: family complaints drive the largest share of CQC notifications in domiciliary care. A good family portal turns those calls into self-service.

What good looks like: visit-confirmation push notifications, carer profile with photo, care-summary feed (filtered for what families should see), MAR access if family wants it, missed-visit alerts that fire automatically rather than after a coordinator notices. No cost per family login.

Ask: "How many of your customers have the family portal active, and what % of their families have logged in this month?" Adoption numbers tell you whether it's a real feature or a marketing one.

7. Carer wellbeing and retention signals

Why it matters: UK home care turnover sits at 28-35% annually (Skills for Care). Replacing a carer costs roughly £3,000-£5,000 in recruitment, induction, training and lost productivity. Software that flags retention risk early pays for itself.

What good looks like: automatic Bradford Factor calculation with sensible RAG thresholds and Equality Act-compliant reason-code exclusions, training-expiry alerts, supervision-overdue flags, missed-shift patterns, and ideally a composite retention-risk score.

Ask: "Show me a carer profile where the system flagged early signs of attrition." If they can't, the analytics are cosmetic.

8. A hands-on trial with your own data, without sales gatekeeping

Why it matters: a demo controlled by the vendor will always look smooth. A hands-on trial with your rota, your carers and your service users is where the platform either holds up or doesn't.

What good looks like: self-service signup (no demo call required to get started), at least 14-30 days of access to the real platform with realistic limits, no automatic charges at trial end, ability to import a sample rota to feel actual workflow. Better still: a genuinely free tier for small agencies, and homecareOS is free up to 5 active carers, 25 service users, 1 office admin, with no time limit.

Ask: "Can I sign up and use the real product today, with my data, without speaking to sales first?" If the answer is no, the platform isn't confident enough to let you try it.

9. Data migration: what they actually import

Why it matters: the data lock-in is real. Most vendors will export, but the question is what the next vendor can import without manual re-keying.

What good looks like: bulk-CSV import for service users, carers, care plans, rate cards and active rotas. Sensible field mapping (not just "match by name"). Migration support included free for the first 30 days. Documented export format for when you eventually leave.

Ask: "If I leave in two years, what data do I get out and in what format?" Vendors who can't answer this cleanly are the ones who lock customers in.

10. Contract flexibility: minimum term and exit

Why it matters: the difference between a 30-day rolling contract and a 36-month minimum-term commitment is the difference between switching costs of zero and switching costs of tens of thousands of pounds.

What good looks like: monthly rolling (or annual with monthly opt-out). Clear exit terms in plain English. No retroactive penalty clauses ("if you cancel you owe the discount back"). Free off-boarding support so the migration doesn't get held hostage.

Ask: "What's the minimum contract, what's the notice period, and what's the financial penalty if I leave at month 6?"

11. Support SLA, and which channel

Why it matters: at 2am on a Sunday when a carer can't check in, "email us, we'll respond within 48 hours" is the wrong answer. Domiciliary care doesn't have weekends off.

What good looks like: phone support during UK office hours minimum, with an emergency out-of-hours route for production-down issues. Chat support inside the app. A status page that updates in real time when there's an incident. Published response-time SLAs by severity.

Ask: "What happened the last time you had a multi-hour outage, and how did you communicate it?" Honest vendors will tell you. Evasive vendors will tell you nothing has ever gone wrong.

12. Integrations: payroll, accounts, telephony, eMAR partners

Why it matters: the platform doesn't operate in isolation. If it doesn't talk to your payroll, you'll spend a day per month moving CSVs around.

What good looks like: CSV export for payroll that matches your bureau's schema (Sage, Xero, Iris, BrightPay). Accounts package integration for invoice posting. Embedded VoIP / SMS for calling carers and service users from the platform. Open API access on higher tiers for the bits you want to automate. Partner integrations with NHS-trust eMAR systems where relevant.

Ask: "Which payroll providers do your customers use most, and what does the export look like?" Look at the actual file. You'll know in 30 seconds whether it's usable.

How to use the checklist in practice

Print this page (the design works in print). Take it to every demo. Score each platform 0-2 on each point as the demo progresses (0 = absent/poor, 1 = present/adequate, 2 = strong). At the end of each demo, total the score out of 24. Anything under 14 falls off the shortlist.

The pattern that works for UK agencies:

  • Week 1: longlist 4-6 vendors using the four-nation cut (point 2) and pricing-model cut (point 4)
  • Weeks 2-3: 30-minute targeted demos against this checklist, three vendors max
  • Weeks 4-5: free trial with the top 1-2, using your real rota and your real carers (point 8)
  • Week 6: contract review and decision

Six weeks of structured evaluation beats six months of demo-shopping every time.
