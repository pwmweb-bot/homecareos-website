Introducing Smart Scheduling: AI-Powered Rota Management

Our latest update brings intelligent rota management to homecareOS. Let the system optimise staff allocation based on skills, location, and availability, without giving up coordinator judgement on the calls that matter.

By Philip Martin · Founder, homecareOS · Published 18 March 2026

The two-thirds problem

Talk to any UK home care coordinator and ask what their day actually looks like. The honest answer, more often than not: "I spent two-thirds of it filling cover." Not building rotas. Not coaching carers. Not improving care plans. Re-shuffling the rota every time a carer calls in sick, runs late, or has a personal emergency.

That work is genuinely hard. To find good cover for a 30-minute visit at 7:15am you need to know: who's free, who's nearby, who already knows this service user, who's got hours headroom this week, who's trained on this person's medication, and who the service user actually likes. In your head, while three other carers are also calling in. Most rota systems hand you a list and say "good luck".

This update changes that. The new AI cover finder does the legwork before the coordinator picks up the phone. The AI rota builder (Pro tier) takes it further, generating an entire week's rota in under 60 seconds, ready for review. Here's what's actually under the hood.

How the AI cover finder works

When a visit becomes uncovered, sickness, sudden cancellation, late running, anything, the system runs every available carer through a five-factor scoring model. The factors and default weights:

FactorWeightWhat it measures
Distance30%Travel time from carer's previous visit (or home, if start of day) to the uncovered visit, via Mapbox routing.
Continuity25%How many visits this carer has done with this service user in the past 90 days. Familiar faces score higher.
Hours headroom20%Capacity left in the carer's contracted hours this week. Avoids creating overtime / availability conflicts.
Preferred carer15%Whether the service user has explicitly flagged this carer as preferred (or excluded them).
Schedule fit10%How well the visit slots into the carer's existing day without breaking another visit's window.

The output is a ranked top-three. The coordinator sees scores like 87 / 82 / 76 with one-line explanations: "12 mins away, 4 prior visits, 8h headroom this week" or "20 mins away, no prior history, but on shift already". They make the final call, the AI just compresses the search space from "everyone available" to "the three most likely matches".

Weights are configurable. An agency that prioritises dementia continuity over travel time can flip the order. The defaults work for most agencies, but the point is they're not hard-coded.

The AI rota builder (Pro tier)

If cover finding is reactive, rota building is proactive. Once a week, usually Wednesday, the AI rota builder takes existing visit patterns, run templates, and carer availability, and generates a complete rota for the upcoming week.

The algorithm does roughly this:

  1. Group visits into geographic clusters using K-means on visit location
  2. For each cluster, identify carers who live within 15 minutes and have hours capacity
  3. Run continuity weighting, visits with high prior history with a specific carer go to that carer first
  4. Solve the remaining visits as a constraint satisfaction problem: minimise total travel time, maximise continuity, respect carer preferences and contractual hours
  5. Surface anything that couldn't be solved (e.g. a visit no carer has capacity for) for coordinator review

For an 80-carer agency with 480 visits in a week, the builder typically returns a draft rota in 45-60 seconds. Around 80% of the visits are placed cleanly; the remaining 20% are flagged for coordinator decisions. The coordinator now spends 90 minutes reviewing instead of 6 hours assembling.

What is, and isn't, actually AI here

"AI" is being used by every home care vendor at the moment, often loosely. To be honest about what we mean:

  • This is AI: the cover-finder scoring model, the rota builder's constraint solver, retention risk scoring (separate feature), and document categorisation. These all involve trained models or scoring algorithms.
  • This is NOT AI (despite the marketing language some vendors use): drag-and-drop rota grids, basic alerts, calendar conflict checks, route optimisation (that's Mapbox routing), or visit time tracking. These are useful, but they're regular software.

The honest answer to "is your rota system AI" is "the cover finder and rota builder are; everything else is good engineering." We think that distinction matters because it sets correct expectations.

A note on AI in care work

Care decisions affect real people. We don't let the algorithm decide alone, every cover suggestion goes through a coordinator, every generated rota is reviewable, every carer can flag a service user they shouldn't be sent to (or vice versa). The AI is a tool that compresses search space; the human is still in charge of the actual allocation.

We also don't use the algorithm for anything punitive. Bradford Factor scoring is calculated separately and surfaced as information, not enforcement. (More on that approach in our Bradford Factor guide.) Carer retention risk scoring exists, but it's surfaced to managers as "have a chat with this person", not "performance manage them out".

What does this replace?

For agencies coming from manual rota systems (whiteboards, Excel, paper diaries), the cover finder typically saves 40-90 minutes per coordinator per day. For agencies coming from older home care platforms without AI cover scoring (Birdie, CarePlanner, Access), the saving is smaller but real, typically 15-30 minutes per coordinator per day, plus better continuity scores because the algorithm consistently surfaces familiar faces. Frequently asked questions

What does "AI rota management" actually do?

In home care, AI rota management means two things in practice: cover finding (scoring every available carer on distance, continuity, hours headroom, preferred-carer flag and schedule fit when a visit becomes uncovered) and rota building (generating a full week's rota from visit patterns and carer availability). Anything else marketed as "AI rota management" is usually marketing language, not algorithmic.

How is the AI cover finder different from a standard rota system?

A standard rota system shows you a list of unallocated visits and a list of available carers, leaving it to the coordinator to manually match them. The AI cover finder pre-computes the match: scoring each available carer against the visit's specific requirements and surfacing the best three options ranked. Coordinators still make the final call.

Does AI rota building replace coordinators?

No. AI rota building generates a starting rota in minutes, typically 60-90% of visits will be in sensible places. Coordinators then review, adjust, and approve. The boring 90% gets automated, the interesting 10% (managing real human situations) is what they still do.

Is AI rota building included in the free plan?

The AI cover finder is included on every plan, including the free starter tier for agencies under 5 active carers. The AI rota builder (which generates a full week's rota in under 60 seconds) is part of the Pro plan at £10/active carer/month, with the office team unmetered.

Can the scoring weights be customised?

Yes. The default weights (distance 30% / continuity 25% / hours headroom 20% / preferred carer 15% / schedule fit 10%) work for most agencies, but every weight is configurable. Some dementia-focused agencies prioritise continuity over distance; some rural agencies do the opposite.
