What home care software actually is

What does home care software do? Home care software runs the day-to-day operations of a domiciliary care agency in one system. It schedules visits, records care plans, daily notes and electronic medication administration records (eMAR), verifies attendance through GPS check-in on a carer's phone, tracks training and background-check expiries, and turns delivered visits into invoices and payroll figures. It replaces separate tools such as paper rotas, spreadsheets, a standalone eMAR app and a billing package.

"Home care software", also called domiciliary care software, home care management software, a domiciliary care management system or just care rostering software, combines workforce scheduling, regulated care records and field-service management in one platform. Its core jobs are:

  • Scheduling visits: matching carers to service users across the week, with travel time between calls, double-ups, shadow shifts, run templates and last-minute cover
  • Care records: digital care plans, daily care notes, body maps, observations, goals and electronic medication administration records (eMAR)
  • Carer mobile app: GPS-verified check-in, offline-capable note taking, MAR signing and document access
  • Compliance: training and background-check (DBS, PVG or AccessNI) expiry tracking, supervisions, audits, incident logs and evidence for your regulator
  • Finance: payer, service and rate management, invoicing (private and council), payroll exports and mileage claims
  • Family access: a portal or app where relatives can see upcoming and completed visits and, where the agency allows it, care notes. Some platforms also let families message the office or query a visit.

One thing home care software isn't: residential care home software. The two are registered as different types of service. In England a home care agency registers with CQC for the regulated activity "personal care", and a care home for "accommodation for persons who require nursing or personal care". Some vendors sell to both markets (Nourish, Log my Care, OneAdvanced), while Birdie, CareLineLive and homecareOS are built specifically for home care. If a vendor pitches one product for both, ask which one its developers invest in most.

Is domiciliary care software the same as home care software?

Yes. Home care software, domiciliary care software, homecare software and a domiciliary care management system all describe the platform an agency uses to plan, deliver and evidence care in people's own homes. "Domiciliary care" is the formal term used in tenders and in registration in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Scotland is the exception: the Care Inspectorate registers the same work as a support service providing care at home. The distinction that does matter is home care versus care home, which we cover in home care vs care home software.

The UK home care software market in 2026

The market splits roughly into three archetypes. Knowing which one a platform belongs to tells you more than any single review.

1. Long-established enterprise suppliers

Large suites from established software groups, aimed at bigger and multi-service providers. They sell separate modules for care planning, rostering, billing and payroll, and they quote prices rather than publish them. Access, for example, lists an annual fee invoiced in advance plus a one-off implementation fee in its G-Cloud 14 pricing (see the pricing table below). Examples: OneAdvanced (IQ Care), which says it has more than 30 years in the care sector, and Access (Access Care Planning, with rostering sold separately as Access People Planner). Person Centred Software is also well established, but its site leads on care homes.

2. Modern cloud platforms

Cloud platforms with carer mobile apps, most of which do not publish a full price list. Pricing models differ: Birdie prices by the care hours you schedule, while CareLineLive and Unique IQ price per carer. Examples: Birdie, CareLineLive, Nourish (which acquired CarePlanner in October 2023) and Unique IQ.

3. Newer challengers

Recent entrants that publish their prices and compete on a specific strength rather than feature breadth. CareCallAI, built in Wales, focuses on Welsh-language support and CIW. homecareOS, founded in 2026, focuses on covering all four UK regulators and on per-carer pricing that never counts office staff who do not deliver visits.

NHS digital social care records and the assured solutions list (England)

In England, NHS England keeps an assured solutions list of digital social care record (DSCR) systems. Listed systems have been assessed as offering the core capabilities a care provider needs. It is separate from regulation: CQC does not endorse any particular record system, and the agency has to assure itself that whatever system it uses meets its needs.

If you hold NHS or council contracts, ask your commissioner whether they expect an assured system before you shortlist. When we checked on 18 September 2026, the list included most of the platforms in our comparison table (see its last column), plus QCS Care Management Software (formerly Carebeans). OneAdvanced, Unique IQ, CareCallAI, QuikPlan, ShiftCare and homecareOS were not on it. The list changes, so check it on the day you decide.

The Data Security and Protection Toolkit (DSPT) is a separate self-assessment the agency completes for itself; assured software does not complete it for you.

What home care management software should do in 2026

Whether a vendor calls it home care management software or a domiciliary care management system, the feature lists use the same headings (scheduling, eMAR, care plans, invoicing), so they are hard to tell apart from the website alone. Here's what to look for in 2026, grouped by category, with what "good" looks like.

Scheduling

  • A true drag-and-drop rota grid, not a list view that calls itself a rota. In the trial, load a full week at your real carer count and check that dragging still responds instantly.
  • Run templates: recurring patterns you can apply once and edit individually
  • Double-ups and shadow shifts as proper roles on the visit (lead carer, second carer, shadow), so pay and billing follow each person
  • Cover finder for last-minute absences: shows who is actually free (not on leave, not already booked) and ranks them on continuity with the client, hours left in their week and travel time
  • Travel time between calls on the rota, and runs you can check on a map (see route planning for domiciliary care)
  • Independent planned, actual, paid and charged times, so what you bill and what you pay the carer can differ

Our guide to building a home care rota goes further.

Carer mobile app

  • Truly offline: care notes captured on a no-signal visit must sync later without data loss
  • Electronic call monitoring (ECM): check-in and check-out verified by GPS, NFC tag or QR code, with a manual override for when a carer has to phone in
  • eMAR with controlled-drug double-sign
  • eMAR that uses NHS dm+d medicine names, so prescriptions match what the pharmacy supplies
  • Body map for wound and skin observations
  • Pay transparency: carers can see their projected earnings and mileage
  • Mobile holiday requests: carers ask for leave in the app and see the answer there. Ask whether every customer has it or only those on a newer version.

Care records and compliance

  • Nation-appropriate evidence templates (see above)
  • Training and DBS/PVG/AccessNI expiry tracking with carer-facing alerts
  • Bradford Factor calculation for absence patterns, with RAG thresholds that match your absence policy and a way to keep pregnancy-related sickness out of the score, which Acas says should not count towards trigger points (the position in Great Britain; Northern Ireland has its own equality law). See our Bradford Factor guide
  • Incident management that flags which incidents need a statutory notification to your regulator and records when it was sent
  • Audit trail: who recorded or corrected a care note or MAR entry, and when. Ask to see the history of a corrected medication record.
  • Reporting: which reports are in the base price, and whether they export

Billing, invoicing and payroll

  • Payer, service and rate-band model: handles 15, 30, 45 and 60-minute bands, fixed-fee live-in and sleeping nights, and council and private rates side by side
  • Invoice generation with the line-level detail councils ask for (local authority billing)
  • Payroll export your payroll package imports without retyping. Ask which packages by name and whether the link is direct or a file you upload.
  • Mileage claims at a rate you set, checked against HMRC's approved mileage allowance payments: for cars and vans in 2026 to 2027, 55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles and 25p after that

More on carer payroll software.

Family portal and commissioner reporting

  • Family portal: ask exactly what relatives can see (visit confirmations, the carer's name and photo, care summaries, medication records) and whether they are alerted to a missed visit.
  • Commissioner reporting: can you give a council or ICB contract monitor planned versus actual visit evidence as an export, without building a spreadsheet by hand? And, if your council runs its own call monitoring system, can the platform send visit times to it?

Ask to see each feature working in the demo. "Coming soon" on a non-negotiable is a red flag.

Questions to ask every vendor before you sign

  1. Who counts as a paid user: carers only, every login, or every service user? And when is the count taken?
  2. Are eMAR, rostering, reporting and the family app in the base price, or add-ons with their own fees?
  3. Is there a minimum monthly fee, minimum term, or onboarding or training fee? (CareLineLive, for example, lists onboarding at up to £300 plus VAT.)
  4. What does it cost to leave, and in what format do you get your data back?
  5. Is there a free plan, and what moves you off it?
  6. In England, is it on NHS England's assured solutions list? Elsewhere, can you show my regulator's fields working?
  7. Which features use AI today, which are on the roadmap, and which third-party AI provider processes our data?
  8. Where is our data stored (country and hosting provider), who are your sub-processors, and will you sign a data processing agreement? Which security certifications do you hold?
  9. Can we speak to two agencies of our size in our nation that have used it for more than a year?

A decision framework for home care agency software

Knowing you need to switch is usually the easy part; telling two platforms apart is harder. Five questions, asked in this order, will narrow the shortlist faster than a round of demo calls.

1. Which regulator are you registered with?

If England: most UK platforms are built for CQC first, so narrow on size, pain and total cost instead. If Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland: ask the vendor for a screenshot of a worked example in your regulatory framework. If they show you CQC dashboards, they're not configured for you.

2. How big is your agency, and what's the trajectory?

  • 1-5 carers: a free tier is realistic, but check what it counts and what it leaves out: homecareOS is free for your first 5 carers, Log my Care's Starter plan for fewer than 10 service users. New agency? See how to start a home care agency in the UK.
  • 6-20 carers: most modern cloud platforms fit. Check the contract length as well as the price: CareLineLive, for example, says its contracts are typically 12 months.
  • 20-60 carers: most modern platforms still fit; enterprise suppliers start to make sense if your council billing is complex.
  • 60+ carers or multi-branch: enterprise territory, or a modern platform with good exports for what it doesn't cover.

3. Where is your operational pain sharpest right now?

  • Last-minute cover: prioritise a cover finder that ranks the carers who are free, plus an offline carer app
  • CQC, CIW, Care Inspectorate or RQIA inspection prep: prioritise compliance dashboards, an audit trail and evidence packs
  • Carer retention: prioritise an easy carer app, holiday requests on the phone and Bradford Factor analytics
  • Council billing: prioritise a billing model that keeps planned, actual, paid and charged times separate, and line-level invoice detail
  • Family complaints or missed visits: prioritise a family portal and automatic missed-visit alerts

4. How often does the product change, and how will you hear about it?

Ask each vendor for its release notes from the last 12 months, and how much notice you get before a change moves a screen your carers use every day.

5. What's the total cost over 2 years, not the headline £/month?

Add: monthly subscription × 24 + implementation fee + migration cost + training cost + integration cost (if you need anything custom) + the cost of any modules not in the base price. Then compare.

Switching home care software: what it involves

A switch is mostly data and people: moving your records across and bringing your carers with you. A sensible order:

  1. Get your data out first. Ask your current supplier, in writing, for an export of service users, care plans, medication records, staff and training records, rate cards and visit patterns, and check the format.
  2. Map rates and patterns before go-live. Check a sample of invoices and payslips from the new system against the old one.
  3. Run both systems side by side briefly. Rota in the new system while the old one stays readable.
  4. Roll out the carer app in small groups. Start with one run, fix what confuses people, then widen, and make sure everyone can check in before the old app goes.
  5. Keep the old records. Agree how you will keep access to, or archive, care records from the old system.

The bottom line

The differences that matter between platforms are practical: which regulator's evidence the platform produces, whether the pricing counts every login or only carers, whether the carer app works offline in a basement flat with no signal, and whether the vendor's "coming soon" list holds things you will use.

Use the five-question framework above to narrow your shortlist to two or three platforms. Then book demos that target your specific pain, not generic walkthroughs, and ask about pricing in writing before you commit. Set a decision date before the first demo, so the evaluation has an end point and does not drift.

Frequently asked questions

What is home care software?

Home care software, also called domiciliary care software or a domiciliary care management system, is the platform a home care agency uses to schedule visits, record care notes and medication, track training and background checks, produce invoices and payroll figures, and give carers a mobile app. It has to hold the records your regulator expects (CQC, CIW, the Care Inspectorate or RQIA), and it differs from care home software because the work is organised around visits, not a building.

What is domiciliary care software?

The same thing as home care software; only the vocabulary differs. The NHS and NICE use both terms for the same service. 'Domiciliary' is more common in formal paperwork: CQC registers domiciliary care services, Welsh law calls it a domiciliary support service and Northern Ireland has Domiciliary Care Agencies Regulations. Scotland is the exception, where the Care Inspectorate registers home care as a support service (care at home).

How much does home care software cost in the UK?

Only 6 of the 15 other UK platforms in our comparison table publish a paid price on their own website, and those that do use different units. Published examples, checked September 2026: TagCare £5 per carer per month; CareLineLive from £8.10 per carer per month with a £120 monthly minimum; Birdie from £200 a month, priced by care hours. homecareOS is free for your first 5 carers, then £10 per carer per month for every carer, with service users and office staff who do not deliver visits never counted. Always ask who counts as a paid user.

What's the best home care software in the UK?

It depends on your nation, size and where your pain is sharpest. On published facts, TagCare and CareLineLive have the lowest per-carer prices, Birdie, Access Care Planning and CareLineLive are on NHS England's assured solutions list, and Log my Care is free under 10 service users. Do I need different software for England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland?

No, but the software has to understand the differences. CQC, CIW, the Care Inspectorate and RQIA each inspect against their own framework. The background check differs (DBS in England and Wales, PVG in Scotland, AccessNI in Northern Ireland), and care workers register with Social Care Wales, the SSSC or NISCC, while England has no equivalent register. Check that a platform handles your regulator, register and background check, and all of them if you work in more than one nation.

Is any home care software approved by CQC?

No. Regulators register and inspect care providers, not software, and CQC says it does not endorse any digital social care record system. In England, NHS England keeps a separate assured solutions list of record systems that meet its core standards; being on it is not a CQC requirement. homecareOS is not on the list. If you hold NHS or council contracts, ask your commissioner whether they expect an assured system.
