Home Care Software UK: The Complete Guide for Domiciliary Agencies

A vendor-honest guide to picking home care software in 2026 — written for UK domiciliary agency owners and managers who've already had a few demos and want to cut through the noise. We'll cover what the category actually is, how the four UK regulators shape what you need, which features genuinely matter in 2026 (and which are marketing), how to read the pricing models, where each of the major platforms lands, and a decision framework you can apply this afternoon.

By Philip Martin · Founder, homecareOS · Published 30 May 2026

What home care software actually is

"Home care software" — also called domiciliary care software, home care management software or just care rostering software — is the operational platform a UK domiciliary agency uses to run the day-to-day. The category sits at the intersection of three things that elsewhere in software are usually separate products: workforce scheduling, regulated clinical record-keeping, and field-service management.

The core jobs of a home care platform are:

  • Scheduling visits — matching carers to service users across the week, handling double-ups, shadow shifts, run templates, and last-minute cover
  • Care records — digital care plans, daily care notes, body maps, observations, goals, electronic medication administration records (eMAR)
  • Carer mobile app — GPS-verified check-in, offline-capable note taking, MAR signing, document access
  • Compliance — training and DBS expiry tracking, supervisions, audits, incident logs, registration-body evidence packs
  • Finance — payer/service/rate management, invoicing (private and council), payroll exports, mileage claims
  • Family and commissioner access — read-only portals so families can see visits and commissioners can verify delivery

One thing home care software isn't: residential care home software. The two get conflated in vendor marketing, but they sit under different regulatory frameworks (RIDDOR vs ASC reporting, Provider Information Return shapes, accommodation regulations) and have different operational shapes. A handful of UK vendors serve both markets with separate products (Nourish, Person Centred Software). Pure home care platforms include Birdie, CareLineLive, CarePlanner, Log my Care, OneAdvanced Care at Home, Unique IQ, and homecareOS. If a vendor pitches you a single product for "care homes and home care", check carefully which one their development team actually invests in.

The UK home care software market in 2026

The market splits roughly into three archetypes. Naming the archetype a platform belongs to is more useful than reading any individual review.

1. Legacy enterprise platforms

Built over a decade or more, designed for multi-branch agencies and local-authority block contracts. Strong on rate-card complexity, council billing, payroll integration. Slower-moving product-wise — large engineering teams but a wide install base that makes change hard. Pricing is usually annual contract, per user, with implementation fees in the £2-10k range. Examples: Access Care Planning, OneAdvanced Care at Home (formerly OneTouch / Personal Care), Person Centred Software.

2. Modern SaaS platforms

Built in the 2010s-early 2020s on modern stacks, mobile-first, monthly billing. Strong on UX, faster product cycles, growing AI feature set. Usually per-user pricing in the £6-£12/user/month range with module add-ons. Examples: Birdie, CareLineLive, CarePlanner, Nourish, Unique IQ.

3. Newer AI-first challengers

Built since 2022-2024, often with AI as a primary feature rather than an add-on. Pricing tends to be lower or genuinely free at the small end. Strong on specific differentiators — AI cover finding, four-nation regulatory coverage, embedded telephony — rather than feature breadth. Examples: Log my Care (free starter tier), CareCallAI, homecareOS.

None of the three archetypes is "better". Which one fits depends on your size, your nation, where your operational pain is sharpest, and how much you value annual-contract stability vs monthly flexibility. We'll come back to that.

The four-nation framework: how UK regulators shape what you need

Almost every "best home care software" listicle ignores this, but it's the single biggest constraint on your shortlist if you operate outside England.

England — CQC

The largest market and the one every vendor builds for first. Inspection framework: the five Key Questions (safe, effective, caring, responsive, well-led). Evidence requirements lean on the Provider Information Return (PIR) and the Quality Statements introduced in the 2023 single assessment framework. DBS is the relevant background-check system. Most platforms have CQC-aligned dashboards out of the box. See our complete CQC compliance guide for the operational detail.

Wales — CIW (Care Inspectorate Wales)

Operates under the Regulation and Inspection of Social Care (Wales) Act 2016, with the Regulated Services (Service Providers and Responsible Individuals) (Wales) Regulations (RISCA) as the rulebook. Inspections issue ratings against four themes (well-being, care and support, environment, leadership and management). DBS is again the background check, but providers must also register staff with Social Care Wales (SCW). Welsh-language considerations apply where the service user prefers Welsh.

Software implication: a platform that only generates CQC-style PIR evidence won't fit. CIW wants its own evidence shape, and SCW registration numbers need their own field separate from DBS. See our dedicated guide to CIW compliance software for Welsh home care agencies.

Scotland — Care Inspectorate

Operates under the Public Services Reform (Scotland) Act 2010 and the Health and Care (Staffing) (Scotland) Act 2019. Inspections rate services against quality indicators on a six-point scale. Background checking is the Protecting Vulnerable Groups (PVG) scheme (administered by Disclosure Scotland), and registered carers must be on the Scottish Social Services Council (SSSC) register.

Software implication: PVG number field, SSSC registration field, and inspection reporting aligned to the quality indicators rather than CQC's Key Questions. For the operational detail, see our guide to Care Inspectorate software for Scottish domiciliary agencies.

Northern Ireland — RQIA

The Regulation and Quality Improvement Authority operates under the Health and Personal Social Services (Quality, Improvement and Regulation) (Northern Ireland) Order 2003. Inspections follow standards on safe and effective care, compassionate care, leadership, and well-led services. Background check is AccessNI (not DBS), and carers must be registered with the Northern Ireland Social Care Council (NISCC).

Software implication: AccessNI field, NISCC registration field, RQIA evidence templates. See our guide to RQIA compliance software for Northern Ireland agencies.

Practical test: Pick the platform's website and search for "RQIA" or "CIW". If the search returns nothing or only marketing copy, that platform is configured for England by default. You can usually still use it, but you'll be force-fitting English regulator fields onto your non-English workflow.

A handful of platforms (homecareOS, CareCallAI) build four-nation coverage in from day one. Most legacy enterprise platforms have nation-specific configurations available but require setup work. Most modern SaaS platforms cover England well, Wales and Scotland partially, and Northern Ireland barely.

Core features that genuinely matter in 2026

The feature lists on vendor sites all look identical because they all copy each other. Here's what to actually look for in 2026, grouped by category, with what "good" looks like.

Scheduling

  • Drag-and-drop rota grid with sub-minute drag performance on a phone-sized agency (60+ carers) — not a list view that calls itself a rota
  • Run templates — recurring patterns you can apply once and edit individually
  • Double-ups and shadow shifts handled as first-class attendee roles, not bolt-on "second carer" flags
  • AI cover finder for last-minute absences — ranks available carers on distance, continuity, hours, preference, schedule fit
  • Independent planned / actual / paid / charged times per visit (essential for council vs private split billing)

Carer mobile app

  • Truly offline — care notes captured on a no-signal visit must sync later without data loss
  • GPS-verified check-in/out with manual override (carers occasionally need to call in)
  • eMAR with controlled-drug double-sign
  • Body map for wound and skin observations
  • Pay transparency — carers can see projected earnings and mileage. This drives retention more than any retention scheme.
  • Mobile holiday requests — Birdie, notably, has had this in Beta with 100+ user votes for months without shipping it. Ask the demo whether it's actually live or "coming soon".

Care records and compliance

  • Nation-appropriate evidence templates (see above)
  • Training and DBS/PVG/AccessNI expiry tracking with carer-facing alerts
  • Bradford Factor calculation for absence patterns (with sensible RAG thresholds and reason-code exclusions for Equality Act compliance — see our Bradford Factor guide)
  • Incident management with regulatory-body submission workflows
  • Audit log covering every change to care records (regulators ask)

Finance

  • Payer / service / rate-band model — handles 15/30/45/60-min bands, fixed-fee live-in, sleeping nights, council vs private rate splits
  • Invoice generation with line-level detail councils accept
  • Payroll exports (Sage, Xero, Iris) — direct integration is rare; CSV that maps to your payroll's import schema is the realistic ask
  • Mileage claims with HMRC-compliant rates (45p first 10,000mi, 25p thereafter)

Family and commissioner portals

  • Family portal — visit confirmations, carer profile (with photo), care summaries, MAR access, missed-visit alerts
  • Commissioner portal — local authority and ICB users verify visits, monitor delivery, approve invoices

Increasingly expected in 2026

  • AI rota builder — generate a draft week from your run templates and constraints; you edit, don't build from scratch
  • Embedded telephony (VoIP) — call any carer or service user from the platform with the call recorded against the contact
  • Earned Wage Access (EWA) — carers can draw earned but unpaid wages mid-pay-period
  • Retention risk scoring — composite signal (Bradford Factor + training lapse + missed visits + supervision overdue) flagging carers likely to leave

None of these are exotic any more. If a vendor's response to "do you have an AI cover finder?" in 2026 is "it's on the roadmap", they're a year behind.

Pricing models compared

UK home care software pricing comes in four shapes. The shape matters as much as the headline £/month — sometimes more.

  • Per user (most common): every login costs, including coordinators, managers, payroll, owners. Typical £6-£12/user/month. The hidden tax for home care is that office staff usually run at 1 per 4-6 carers — so the bill is 25-50% bigger than the carer count alone would suggest.
  • Per active service user: Birdie's headline model. Scales with how many people you care for, not with how many carers you employ. Predictable if your service-user count is steady; punishing if you take on a council block contract that doubles overnight.
  • Per active carer (homecareOS's model): only the carers who delivered at least one visit in the month count. Office team is free. Tracks operational reality (visits delivered = revenue).
  • Per bed / per resident: residential-care framing that some platforms try to apply to home care. Genuinely doesn't fit. Avoid.

We've written a separate deep-dive on per-user vs per-carer pricing with a worked example showing the gap at a typical 12-carer agency. Short version: a 12-carer agency with 4 office staff pays for 16 seats under per-user, 12 under per-carer.

Other things to ask any vendor on pricing:

  1. Are eMAR, scheduling and reporting in the base price, or are they modules with their own fees?
  2. Do family-portal or commissioner-portal logins cost extra?
  3. Is there a minimum contract? (Monthly rolling vs 12-month vs multi-year)
  4. What's the off-boarding cost if you leave in six months?
  5. Is there a free tier, and if so, what triggers an automatic move off it?

A decision framework: how to choose

Most agency owners we talk to know they need to switch but can't tell whether platform A or platform B is actually better for them. Five questions in this order will narrow the shortlist faster than a hundred demo calls.

1. Which regulator are you registered with?

If England: every UK platform on the market is a candidate. If Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland: ask the vendor for a screenshot of a worked example in your regulatory framework. If they show you CQC dashboards, they're not configured for you.

2. How big is your agency, and what's the trajectory?

  • 1-5 active carers: a free tier is realistic. homecareOS Free, Log my Care free tier.
  • 6-20 active carers: the modern SaaS sweet spot. Per-user or per-carer pricing, monthly rolling, mobile-first.
  • 20-60 active carers: mid-market. Most modern SaaS still fits; legacy enterprise begins to make sense if you have complex council billing.
  • 60+ active carers / multi-branch: legacy enterprise territory. Or modern SaaS with strong API access for the bits it doesn't natively cover.

3. Where is your operational pain sharpest right now?

Be honest with yourself. Is it:

  • Last-minute cover → prioritise AI cover finder + offline mobile app
  • CQC/CIW/CI/RQIA inspection prep → prioritise compliance dashboards + audit log + evidence packs
  • Carer retention → prioritise pay transparency, mobile UX, holiday requests on phone, Bradford Factor analytics
  • Council billing complexity → prioritise the rate-card / four-time-value billing model + invoice line detail
  • Family complaints / missed visits → prioritise family portal + automated missed-visit alerts

4. What's your roadmap appetite?

Legacy enterprise platforms change slowly — predictable but you wait years for new features. Modern SaaS ships monthly but breaks workflows sometimes. Newer challengers ship weekly but might pivot. Pick the cadence you can live with.

5. What's the total cost over 2 years, not the headline £/month?

Add: monthly subscription × 24 + implementation fee + migration cost + training cost + integration cost (if you need anything custom) + the cost of any modules not in the base price. Then compare. Headline £/month rarely tells the truth.

Where each major UK platform lands

A one-paragraph honest read on each of the 14 platforms we maintain side-by-side comparisons for. Click any name for the full comparison.

  • Birdie — modern SaaS leader. Strong UX, large customer base, well-funded. Per-active-service-user pricing plus office-user fees can stack. Holiday requests on mobile is still in Beta (117 user votes, no GA date).
  • Access Care Planning (formerly Mobizio / Servelec) — established enterprise. Strong on council billing complexity, multi-branch. Higher TCO, longer implementation, per-user pricing.
  • Log my Care — free starter for care notes only. Paid plan adds scheduling and eMAR as per-user add-ons. Strong fit for very small agencies starting their digital transition.
  • CareLineLive — modern SaaS, good UK customer base, per-user pricing. Solid generalist; fewer differentiators.
  • Person Centred Software — primarily residential but with a domiciliary product. Strong on care notes; weaker on the scheduling and finance side a pure home care agency needs.
  • Nourish — same as PCS: residential-first, domiciliary as a secondary product line.
  • OneAdvanced (formerly OneTouch / Personal Care) — enterprise scale, annual contracts, per-user pricing with module bundles.
  • Unique IQ — established UK player, per-user, generalist.
  • CareCallAI — newer challenger competing on AI features and four-nation coverage. Direct overlap with homecareOS's positioning.
  • Care Control — long-established, very feature-rich, perpetual-licence-style commercial model that's unusual in modern SaaS.
  • everyLIFE — PASS / PASS Genie product line, strong on integrations with care home and supported-living settings.
  • QuikPlan — scheduling-focused, lighter on the records/compliance side.
  • ShiftCare — international (originally AU) competing in UK with per-user pricing.
  • TagCare — newer, RFID-tag-based attendance verification as the differentiator.

The bottom line

Picking UK home care software is one of those decisions where the marketing makes everything look interchangeable and the demos make everything look identical. The differences that actually matter are the unglamorous ones: which regulator's evidence shape the platform actually generates, whether the pricing model counts every login or just the field carers, whether the mobile app works offline when your carer is in a basement flat with no signal, and whether the vendor's "coming soon" list contains things you'll genuinely use.

Use the five-question framework above to narrow your shortlist to two or three platforms. Then book demos that target your specific pain — not generic walkthroughs — and ask about pricing in writing before you commit. The agencies that get this right do it in six weeks of careful evaluation, not six months of demo-shopping.
