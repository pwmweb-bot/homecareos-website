Every Report

Fifteen reports, grouped the way you already think

Not a report builder and a blank page. Fifteen reports that already know what a home care agency gets asked for, in four groups. Every one takes a date range, and the range follows you from one report to the next rather than resetting each time.

Operations

  • Operational. Visit completion, how close visits run to plan, and carer utilisation.
  • Demand vs capacity. Visit hours against contracted carer hours, so recruitment is planned rather than reactive.
  • Round efficiency. Travel time between calls, and what share of the working day it takes.
  • Clock-in methods. GPS, QR and NFC tag setup, for each service user.

Care quality

  • Incidents. Rate, severity and cause over time, and whether the service is genuinely getting better or worse.
  • Care quality. Risk assessments, medication compliance, incidents and goals in one view.
  • Outcomes and goals. Goal progress and achievement, by category.
  • Care continuity. How consistent a team each person actually sees.
  • Record completeness. What each service user record holds, and what is missing from it.
  • Medication coverage. Which scheduled doses have a visit to cover them, by client and by day.

People

  • Training matrix. Every course against every member of staff, and what is outstanding.
  • Compliance. Background checks, right to work, training and supervision.
  • Absence. Bradford Factor, sickness trends and leave.

Money

  • Pay matrix. What every carer is paid an hour, and where that rate comes from.
  • Revenue and pay. Revenue by payer, invoice aging and pay cost by role.

Every report exports to CSV. The incidents report also produces a Quality and Safety pack as a PDF.

A handful of causes explain most of your incidents

The incidents report sorts causes largest first and runs a cumulative line across them. Where that line passes eighty per cent is where your attention belongs, and it is usually two or three causes rather than the whole list.

  • Largest cause
  • Inside the top eighty per cent
  • The long tail

How close your visits run to the time you planned

Every completed visit has a planned length and an actual one. This is the spread of the difference between the two, in minutes: how much each visit ran over or under the time it was given. It tells you something an average cannot, namely whether your call times are realistic or whether carers are quietly absorbing the gap.

  • Within fifteen minutes of plan
  • Running long, or finishing early
  • Finished early
  • Bang on the planned time
  • Ran over

Horizontal axis: actual visit length minus planned visit length, in minutes.

A tail on the right is a call that needs more time on the rota. A tail on the left is a call that may not need the time it is billed for. Both are worth knowing before a commissioner asks.

Three ways to clock in, and a report on which gets used

Carers check in by GPS, by scanning a QR code in the home, or by tapping an NFC tag. The clock-in report shows the mix, so you can see where GPS is struggling and which homes would be better with a tag on the wall.

  • GPS
  • QR code
  • NFC tag
  • Manual override

The proportions shown here are illustrative. Your own mix is whatever your carers and their homes actually produce.
