A search for the best domiciliary care software in the UK returns a lot of lists, and very few of them show where the prices came from. This one only uses figures a supplier has published on its own website, checked on 13 October 2026. Where a supplier does not publish a price, the table says so. It does not guess.

homecareOS is our product, so it is in the table with the others. It is not declared the winner. For a five-carer agency and a 40-carer agency the cheapest system is often a different one, because the bills are calculated in different units.

## Start with the unit, not the headline price

The Department of Health and Social Care tells providers that a supplier's monthly charge may be based on staff, on core system users, or on clients or bed capacity, and that hardware, setup and training may sit outside that figure. That is the useful frame for this market.

You will see at least four units on UK domiciliary shortlists:

- **Per carer.** The bill follows the number of people delivering visits. Ask whether office staff are included, and whether a carer who works one hour a week still counts.
- **Per scheduled hour.** The bill follows the care you put on the rota. It can track revenue. It also rises when you take on more hours, even if you have not hired another coordinator.
- **Per service user.** The bill follows the number of people you support. A live-in package and a 15-minute call can cost the same in software if both count as one person.
- **On request.** No public list price. A written quote is the only figure you should compare.

Ask for the quote in writing, excluding VAT and then including it, with minimums, contract length, onboarding, and any module that is not in the base price. [Digital social care records: choosing and implementing solutions](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/digital-social-care-records-choosing-and-implementing-solutions) sets out that buying checklist for care records. It applies just as well to the rota and the invoice.

## What the price does not tell you

A low monthly figure is not a care record. [Regulation 17](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-regulation/providers/regulations-service-providers-and-managers/health-social-care-act/regulation-17) of the Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014 requires an accurate, complete and contemporaneous record of each person's care and of the decisions taken about it. CQC's guidance says those records should be complete, legible, indelible, accurate and up to date. The regulation does not name a software brand. It does describe the record you will be asked to produce.

[Regulation 9](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-regulation/providers/regulations-service-providers-and-managers/health-social-care-act/regulation-9) is the person-centred care duty: care that is appropriate, meets needs and reflects preferences, planned with the person. The software question is whether the plan, the goals and the visit notes stay attached to each other. That is covered in [care planning software for UK home care](/blog/care-planning-software-uk.html).

If you hold a council contract, ask whether you must use the council's electronic call monitoring system, your own, or both. Newham and Lambeth have both published contract notices that make visit monitoring part of how they check hours and pay providers. The detail is in [electronic call monitoring for home care](/blog/electronic-call-monitoring-home-care.html).

For the care record itself, use the [NHS England assured solutions list](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/digital-social-care-records-choosing-and-implementing-solutions). The Department of Health and Social Care says every solution on it offers care planning, recording, reporting and audit trails, meets national quality and security standards, and can connect with GP records. The list is not a ranking, and it changes. Check it on the day you buy. Do not take a supplier's word for membership without looking.

GP Connect has its own conditions on that same page. A provider needs an assured digital social care record that integrates with GP Connect, 'standards met' on the Data Security and Protection Toolkit, and a signed National Data Sharing Agreement. On the [homecareOS integrations page](/integrations.html), GP Connect is listed among connectors agencies can ask for. It is not described there as a live connection.

## A shortlist you can explain to a board

1. Write down how you are priced today: carers, office users, service users, and scheduled hours in a normal week.
2. Put each supplier's public unit next to that. If there is no public price, leave the cell blank until the quote arrives.
3. Add VAT, the minimum fee, the contract length, and anything sold as an add-on. Rostering and eMAR are add-ons on at least one of the plans above.
4. Ask for one week of your real visits to be scheduled in a trial, including a missed call and a medication that was refused.
5. Read the export terms before you sign. You will want the care plans and the visit history if you leave.

Skills for Care's [state of the adult social care sector and workforce in England, 2025](https://www.skillsforcare.org.uk/Adult-Social-Care-Workforce-Data/workforceintelligence/resources/Reports/National/The-state-of-the-adult-social-care-sector-and-workforce-in-England-2025.pdf) reports that, as at March 2025, CQC regulated 15,232 non-residential locations in England, up from 8,856 in 2018. In 2024/25 those services had an estimated 595,000 filled posts and 59,000 vacant posts, a vacancy rate of 9.9%. Software will not fill those posts. It does decide how much of a coordinator's day is spent rebuilding a rota around them.
