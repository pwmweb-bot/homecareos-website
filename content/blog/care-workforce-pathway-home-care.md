Ask a carer where their job leads and many will shrug. Senior carer, maybe care co-ordinator, then it gets vague. That vagueness costs agencies: people who cannot see a future in care are easier to lose to the supermarket down the road.

The **Care Workforce Pathway** is the government's answer to that problem. It sets out, role by role, what people in adult social care should know, be able to do and value, and how they can develop and progress. It was first published in January 2024, expanded in April 2025, and expanded again in July 2026, so many agencies have not yet looked at the current version.

This guide is for registered managers, owners and training leads in home care agencies in England. It explains what the pathway contains, how its role categories map onto the jobs in a domiciliary care agency, and practical ways to use it from the job advert to the appraisal. It is general guidance based on the GOV.UK and Skills for Care pages, not legal advice.

## What the Care Workforce Pathway is

GOV.UK's [Care workforce pathway for adult social care](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/care-workforce-pathway-for-adult-social-care) is published by the Department of Health and Social Care (DHSC). The [overview](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/care-workforce-pathway-for-adult-social-care/care-workforce-pathway-for-adult-social-care-overview) says it:

- sets out the knowledge, skills, values and behaviours needed to work in adult social care in England
- provides a universal career structure for the sector's 1.5 million workers, which DHSC describes as the first of its kind
- was co-developed by DHSC with Skills for Care, care providers, workforce representatives and people with lived experience of care
- now covers nearly all roles in the sector that are not regulated health or social work roles

The pathway applies to England. It covers people directly employed by adult social care providers, so it does not include care workers employed by local councils or in health settings.

It is not the same as the Care Certificate. The Care Certificate is the induction standard for new care workers. The pathway is the wider career structure, and its new to care role category builds on Care Certificate learning. Our guide to [the Care Certificate for home care agencies](/blog/care-certificate-home-care-agencies.html) covers induction in detail.

## The 18 role categories

The overview lists 11 **direct** role categories and 7 **supporting** role categories. Each category describes a group of similar roles, because different agencies use different job titles.

| Role category | How GOV.UK describes it |
| --- | --- |
| New to care | Would normally have been in their first care role for up to 12 months |
| Care or support worker | Established in their role, with consolidated skills and experience |
| Activity co-ordinator | Supports people to lead full, meaningful and healthy lives through activities |
| Enhanced care worker | Delegated healthcare activities by regulated professionals and/or provides specialist condition-specific support |
| Care technologist | Uses technology to improve care and support |
| Personal assistant | Supports an individual to live more independently, usually in their own home |
| Supervisor or leader | Might be a team leader with some staff management responsibilities |
| Practice leader | Practises a care specialism and helps others develop theirs, without line management responsibilities |
| Deputy manager | Has staff management responsibilities and helps to run the service |
| Registered manager | Focuses on regulatory and legal requirements and runs the service |
| Nominated individual | A senior individual who holds oversight responsibilities and supports governance |

The supporting role categories are corporate management, learning and development practitioner, quality assurance lead, administration, catering, domestic and maintenance. They are described in less detail than the direct categories.

## How the pathway maps to jobs in a home care agency

The pathway deliberately avoids fixed job titles, so the first job for an agency is to map its own roles onto the categories. A typical domiciliary care agency might map like this. Treat it as a starting point and check each role's actual duties against the GOV.UK role category pages.

- **New carers in their first year** sit in **new to care**. GOV.UK says this category is a starting point for people with no experience and focuses on building a foundational level of competence to ensure safe practice.
- **Experienced carers** sit in **care or support worker**. The [care or support worker page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/care-workforce-pathway-for-adult-social-care/care-or-support-worker-role-category) says progression is towards a Level 3 standard, potentially through a diploma or apprenticeship route.
- **Carers who take on delegated healthcare tasks**, such as tasks a community nurse has trained and signed them off to do, are likely to fit **enhanced care worker**.
- **Senior carers, field care supervisors and team leaders** are likely to fit **supervisor or leader**. The [supervisor or leader page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/care-workforce-pathway-for-adult-social-care/supervisor-or-leader-role-category) says that at this level someone might have some people management responsibilities and be considering whether to develop their practice or go into management.
- **Your dementia, end of life or moving and handling champion** may fit **practice leader**, if they lead a specialism without line management.
- **Office staff** such as care co-ordinators and administrators sit in **administration**. GOV.UK's administration page lists care co-ordinator among its example role titles.
- **Deputy manager, registered manager and nominated individual** each have their own category.

Mapping often shows gaps. An agency might find it has many carers in the first two categories and a large jump to the office, with no clear step in between. That gap is useful information for retention planning.

## The values behind every role

The pathway includes a set of universal sector values, developed by Think Local Act Personal (TLAP) with people who draw on care and support. The overview lists them as:

- kind, compassionate and empathetic
- honest, trustworthy and reliable
- respect
- courageous and principled
- see the whole person
- flexible, open and learning
- proud and positive

The overview's own example is a home care client, Ann, who explains that things go wrong when carers arrive acting as though the job is a series of tasks, and go well when they take time to know her as a whole person. GOV.UK says organisations do not have to replace values that already work, but suggests considering the pathway values, for example as the basis for recruitment.

## Using the pathway in recruitment

The pathway gives you a shared, published language for what a good carer looks like. Practical uses:

1. **Job adverts.** Use the role category description and two or three of the values to describe the job in plain words. Our guide to [writing a care worker job advert](/blog/care-worker-job-advert.html) covers structure and wording.
2. **Interview questions.** Build questions around the values, such as asking for an example of seeing the whole person rather than the task list, or of speaking up when something was not right. See our [care worker interview questions](/blog/care-worker-interview-questions.html) and [values-based recruitment](/blog/values-based-recruitment-social-care.html) guides.
3. **Recruiting people new to care.** The new to care category makes it clear that a first job in care is expected to start with foundational learning, not experience. That supports agencies [recruiting care workers with no experience](/blog/recruiting-care-workers-no-experience.html).
4. **Showing a future.** Applicants can see where the job could lead. Mentioning progression routes in your advert and at interview is a simple way to stand out.

The pathway does not replace the legal pre-employment checks. CQC's Regulation 19 still requires fit and proper persons, with the checks set out in our [care worker recruitment checklist](/blog/care-worker-recruitment-checklist.html).

## Using the pathway in induction, supervision and training

CQC's [Regulation 18: Staffing](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-regulation/providers/regulations-service-providers-and-managers/health-social-care-act/regulation-18) requires staff to receive the support, training, professional development, supervision and appraisal they need to do their job. The pathway gives that a structure:

- **Induction.** Use the new to care category to check your induction covers the minimum knowledge and skills for safe practice, alongside the Care Certificate. Our guide to [onboarding new carers](/blog/onboarding-new-carers-home-care.html) covers the steps from offer to first visit.
- **Supervision and appraisal.** Use the carer's current role category as the reference point for development conversations. Skills for Care's [Empowering managers and leaders](https://www.skillsforcare.org.uk/Developing-your-workforce/Care-workforce-pathway/Empowering-managers-and-leaders.aspx) resources suggest helping staff set career goals through meaningful career conversations and coaching style questions. See our guide to [supervision and appraisal for care workers](/blog/supervision-appraisal-care-workers.html).
- **Training plans.** Each direct role category includes specific areas of practice and suggested learning opportunities. GOV.UK notes that agencies should define their own setting-specific training, such as dementia training where the people you support need it. Our [mandatory training guide](/blog/mandatory-training-care-workers.html) covers the baseline.
- **Funding.** The overview links the pathway to the [Learning and Development Support Scheme (LDSS)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/adult-social-care-learning-and-development-support-scheme/learning-and-development-support-scheme-for-the-adult-social-care-workforce-a-guide-for-employers), which lets eligible employers claim towards certain courses and qualifications for eligible staff, including the Level 2 Adult Social Care Certificate. The overview says funding is for eligible courses completed by the end of February 2027, so check the current GOV.UK guidance on eligibility, caps and claim deadlines before you rely on it.

It also helps to be clear that development is not only promotion. GOV.UK says the pathway is designed to show how people can develop skills within a role category as well as progress to more senior positions. Plenty of excellent carers want to stay carers, and recognising specialist skills is a way to value them.

## Using the pathway for retention

The overview says employers who have implemented the pathway since 2024 have reported more confident managers, staff feeling more valued and clearer about their development, more consistent appraisals, improved recruitment and retention, and reduced workforce churn. These are reported experiences from adopting employers, not a guarantee, but they match what agencies already know: carers who can see a future and feel recognised are more likely to stay.

Simple ways to make the pathway visible to your team:

- show each carer which role category they are in now, and what the next step could look like
- link pay steps, titles or recognition to moving between categories, if your budget allows
- give senior carers and champions a named role that matches the supervisor or leader or practice leader categories
- talk about the pathway in supervision, not only at appraisal

For more on keeping carers, see our guides to [care worker retention ideas](/blog/care-worker-retention-ideas.html), [reducing carer turnover](/blog/carer-turnover-home-care.html) and [employee engagement in home care](/blog/employee-engagement-home-care.html).

## A simple way to start

1. **List your roles** and their real duties, including office roles.
2. **Map each role** to a pathway role category using the GOV.UK role category pages.
3. **Compare** your induction, training matrix and supervision templates with the knowledge and skills for each category.
4. **Spot the gaps**, such as no step between carer and office, or no recognition for specialist skills.
5. **Tell your team**, and use the categories in adverts, interviews and supervision from now on.
