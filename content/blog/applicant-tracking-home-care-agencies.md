Most home care agencies do not fail safer recruitment because they have never heard of a DBS check. They fail because the evidence is scattered: an application in email, a right to work photo on a phone, a reference PDF in Downloads, a DBS number in a notebook, and a spreadsheet that says "cleared" with no date.

Applicant tracking is the antidote. It means every applicant has one record, a clear stage, and the documents for that stage attached to it. When CQC samples a staff file under [Regulation 19](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2014/2936/regulation/19) and [Schedule 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2014/2936/schedule/3), you open one person, not five folders.

This guide is for registered managers who are still running recruitment from inboxes and shared drives. It is not a vendor bake-off. For the checklist of checks themselves, see our [care worker recruitment checklist](/blog/care-worker-recruitment-checklist.html). For ideas on attracting applicants, see [how to recruit care workers in the UK](/blog/how-to-recruit-care-workers-uk.html).

## What applicant tracking actually is

In a domiciliary care agency, applicant tracking is:

- A **named record** for each person who applies or is invited.
- A **stage** that matches your safer recruitment policy, not a vague "in progress".
- **Documents and checks** stored against that record: identity, right to work, employment history, references, DBS, health questionnaire, interview notes, offer.
- An **audit trail**: who invited them, who chased the reference, when the DBS number was recorded.
- A way to turn a successful applicant into a **staff file** without re-typing everything.

It is the operational half of the recruitment policy CQC asks to see in its [recruitment policy and procedures](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-regulation/registration/supporting-documents-provider/document/recruitment-policy-procedures) supporting document. The policy says what stages you run. The tracking system shows that you ran them.

## Why spreadsheets fail

Spreadsheets are fine for a headcount. They are a poor staff file.

| What an inspector asks for | What a spreadsheet usually shows |
| --- | --- |
| Full employment history with gaps explained | A tick in a "CV received" column |
| Right to work check, dated, with the method used | "RTW OK" in green |
| Two references, including previous care employment where required | "Refs chased" |
| DBS certificate number, level, issue date, barred list | "DBS clear" |
| Health questionnaire and follow-up | Blank, or a note saying "on paper" |
| Who decided to appoint, and when | The manager's memory |

The legal duty is not to colour a cell. It is to employ fit and proper people and to keep the information in Schedule 3 available. CQC's [guidance on Regulation 19](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-regulation/providers/regulations-service-providers-and-managers/health-social-care-act/regulation-19) is written around that evidence. A spreadsheet that cannot produce the file fails the test even when every applicant was honest.

Shared inboxes fail for the same reason. The person who knows where the second reference went is on annual leave. The WhatsApp photo of a passport is not a controlled right to work check under the Home Office [employer's guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide).

## Stages that match safer recruitment

A useful pipeline mirrors the checks, not the sales jargon of a generic HR product. For home care in England, seven stages cover the ground most agencies need:

1. **Application** — form complete, employment history present, gaps explained.
2. **Documents** — identity, photograph, certificates, proof of address as your policy requires.
3. **References** — requested, received, satisfactory, filed.
4. **DBS** — correct level applied for, certificate seen, number and dates recorded. Detail in our [DBS guide](/blog/dbs-check-for-care-workers.html).
5. **Interview** — values-based questions scored and retained.
6. **Offer** — conditional or firm, with outstanding checks named.
7. **Onboarding** — induction, mandatory training plan, shadow shifts, readiness for the rota.

Right to work and the health questionnaire sit inside that flow as documents and checks, not as afterthoughts. The Home Office pages on [checking a job applicant's right to work](https://www.gov.uk/check-job-applicant-right-to-work) set the methods. Your tracking record should show which method you used and when.

Skills for Care's [recruitment and retention](https://www.skillsforcare.org.uk/Recruitment-and-retention/Recruitment-and-retention.aspx) work also stresses speed and a good candidate experience. A visible stage helps: applicants who can see what is left to do chase fewer "any update?" emails, and managers can see where the bottleneck is.

## What good looks like on a Tuesday afternoon

Imagine an inspector asks for the file of a carer who started six weeks ago. With proper applicant tracking you should be able to show, in one place:

- The original application and employment history.
- The right to work check and identity documents.
- Both references and any explanation you sought about previous care employment ending.
- The DBS level, certificate number, issue date and your decision.
- Interview notes and the offer letter.
- The health questionnaire and any occupational health follow-up.
- The date they were converted to a carer record and when they were added to the rota.

That is the same story told in our older guide to [staff file requirements](/blog/safer-recruitment-staff-files-uk.html), with a system behind it so the story is not rebuilt from memory.
