Safer recruitment in domiciliary care is how a registered manager decides who is fit to walk into someone else's home alone. It is not a folder label. For agencies in England it sits under [Regulation 19](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2014/2936/regulation/19) of the Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, [Schedule 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2014/2936/schedule/3), and [CQC's guidance on Regulation 19](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-regulation/providers/regulations-service-providers-and-managers/health-social-care-act/regulation-19).

This post is about the **recruitment process** CQC expects. It is not a duplicate of our [staff file requirements](/blog/safer-recruitment-staff-files-uk.html) guide, which lists what must sit on the file once the checks are done. For the practical pack (right to work, DBS, references, health questionnaire), use the [care worker recruitment checklist](/blog/care-worker-recruitment-checklist.html). For attracting applicants and running a pipeline, see [how to recruit care workers](/blog/how-to-recruit-care-workers-uk.html) and [applicant tracking for home care agencies](/blog/applicant-tracking-home-care-agencies.html).

## What Regulation 19 actually requires

Regulation 19 is the fit and proper persons employed duty. People employed for a regulated activity must:

- be of **good character**
- have the **qualifications, competence, skills and experience** necessary for the work
- be able, by reason of their **health** after reasonable adjustments, to perform the tasks that are intrinsic to the role

Providers must **establish and operate** recruitment procedures that make those conditions true. Schedule 3 information must be **available** for each person employed, and CQC's guidance says that information must be **confirmed before they are employed**. Where someone later stops meeting the criteria, regulation 19(5) requires necessary and proportionate action, and referral to a professional regulator where that applies.

"Person employed" is wider than payroll. CQC's guidance says it includes volunteers, contractors, and agency or bank staff. The full employment history in Schedule 3 does not apply to most volunteers, except where the volunteer is a provider, director or registered manager.

CQC cannot prosecute for a breach of Regulation 19 itself, but it can take regulatory action, and it must refuse registration if a provider cannot satisfy CQC that they can and will comply.

## What CQC wants to see in your recruitment policy

When you register, CQC asks for a [recruitment policy and procedures](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-regulation/registration/supporting-documents-provider/document/recruitment-policy-procedures) supporting document. That page is a clear map of the process inspectors expect you to operate, not only to write down.

Your policy should show:

- how recruitment is **fair** and complies with the law
- the **stages** of the process — for example advert, application, shortlisting, interview, checking identity, taking up references, DBS check, offering the post
- what you check at **each** stage
- your approach to **volunteers and apprentices** where they apply
- a recruitment **complaints** procedure
- what **references** you require
- how you check **previous employment history**, especially health or social care work or work with children or vulnerable adults
- how you verify **why previous employment ended**
- any separate approach to **sponsoring overseas workers**

It should also show how you comply with the Equality Act 2010, UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018. Pre-employment health questions belong in that Equality Act frame: gather what you need for the intrinsic tasks of the role, after reasonable adjustments, without turning the interview into an irrelevant medical interrogation.

## Good character, competence and ongoing fitness

Good character is not a vibes test. CQC expects robust processes that gather available information and have regard to honesty, trust, reliability and respect. If information later suggests someone is not of good character, you must act. If you appoint despite Schedule 3 concerns, record your reasons.

Competence sits beside character. Providers must assess competence **before someone works unsupervised**, and provide appropriate supervision until that assessment is done. CQC expects providers that employ healthcare assistants and social care support workers to follow the **Care Certificate** standards when assessing competence. A person may join with the expectation that they will become competent within a set timeframe while training, but that does not mean they should be left alone on a first visit before your own competence gate is met. Our [Care Certificate guide](/blog/care-certificate-home-care-agencies.html) covers induction evidence in more detail.

Fitness is not a one-off. Regulation 19(5) and CQC's guidance require regular review of employees' fitness and a fair, timely response when concerns arise, including interim measures if people using the service could be at risk.

## Why domiciliary care makes the process sharper

In a care home, colleagues are usually on the same floor. In domiciliary care, a new worker often goes alone into a private home. That is why values and suitability at interview matter as much as the paperwork, and why your policy should be clear that Schedule 3 checks and competence assessment come **before** unsupervised visits.

Interview questions should test honesty about history, understanding of dignity and safeguarding, and how the person would handle a closed front door, a medication prompt, or a family dispute — without inventing a “pass mark” that is not in your policy. Shortlisting and interview design should still meet the Equality Act.

DBS is one stage, not the whole process. For eligibility and certificate handling, use the [DBS check for care workers](/blog/dbs-check-for-care-workers.html) guide. Safer recruitment still needs identity, employment history, explanations for gaps, references, verification of why relevant previous employment ended, health information relevant to the role, and any qualification evidence Schedule 3 expects.

If another party (an agency or umbrella body) runs checks, CQC still expects you to assure yourself that those checks are complete and satisfactory before the person works for you.
