Home / Migration

Moving off your current system, without losing the last five years

The thing that keeps agencies on software they have outgrown is rarely the software. It is the fear of the move. Here is exactly what comes across, who does the work, and what happens if it goes wrong.

What Comes Across

Seven things move, and they keep their relationships

Not a flat dump of contacts. Visits stay attached to the right service user and the right carer, and training and background checks stay attached to the person they belong to.

Your people

  • Carers, with contact details and start dates
  • Service users, with addresses and care details
  • Worker registrations for SCW, SSSC and NISCC

Your operation

  • Visits and rota history
  • Care plans

Your compliance evidence

  • Background checks including DBS, with numbers and expiry dates
  • Training records, providers, completion and expiry

Everything is imported from CSV, which is the one format nearly every home care platform can produce, whether or not it wants to help you leave.

Who Does It

We run the migration, you check it

You are not handed an importer and wished well. Today the homecareOS team runs migrations directly: you send the exports, we map your columns onto our fields, and you look at a preview of exactly what will be created before anything is committed.

That preview matters. Field names differ between platforms, dates arrive in three different formats, and names come through as "Smith, Jane" as often as "Jane Smith". Those are the things that quietly corrupt an import, and they are the things a preview catches.

Self-service migration is on the roadmap. It is not how it works today, and we would rather say so than imply you will be doing this alone at eleven at night.

If It Goes Wrong

Every import can be undone

Each row created by a migration is stamped with the import that created it. If something is not right, the whole import is reversed in one step rather than unpicked by hand.

The record of what was attempted survives the undo, so a second attempt is informed by the first instead of starting blind. In practice this changes the emotional temperature of the whole exercise: an import becomes something you try, look at, and redo if needed.

There is also no size limit. The licence prices carers rather than capping them, so an import is never refused because your agency is too big or your history too long.

What It Costs

Your live data comes across free

Two different jobs, priced differently, and it is worth knowing which one you are asking for.

Live data, free

Your current carers, current service users, their care plans and the running rota. This is everything you need to open homecareOS on a Monday morning and carry on working, and there is no charge for it.

Plenty of the market prices this. Access publish implementation fees on their G-Cloud listing running from £720 to several thousand depending on the product and tier, and everyLIFE file a typical one off fee of £3,600 for implementation and training. We would rather not put a toll on the way in. A charge to get your data into a system sits uncomfortably close to a charge to get it out, and one of those is the thing agencies fear most about switching.

History, quoted on what is obtainable

Past service users, staff who have left, and old visit and care records are a separate job. It is quoted rather than fixed, and the reason is worth saying plainly: the price is set by what your current provider will actually release, not by how much history you have.

A clean CSV export of historic records is quick work whether it covers one year or six. A provider who will only produce PDFs, or who makes you open records one at a time on screen, is slow work at any volume. So we ask for a sample first, at no charge, and quote from what is really there rather than from a band on a price list.

It is also optional. Your statutory retention duty can be met by keeping your old provider's export, so bringing history into the new system is a convenience rather than a requirement.

Before You Move

What to ask your current provider for

This is the part agencies leave too late. Ask for it early, in writing, because export requests have a way of quietly consuming a notice period.

Ask for a full CSV export of

  • Clients or service users
  • Staff or carers
  • The rota and visit history, with dates and times
  • Care plans
  • Training records
  • Background checks

Know where you stand

Under UK GDPR you have a right to receive personal data you have provided in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format. The records your agency has put into a system are your operational history, not a hostage.

If a provider will only offer PDFs, or charges an unexpected fee to release your own data, that is worth knowing before you give notice rather than after.
